## W01 · D1 – Sepsis-3, SOFA and SOFA-2, screening

**1.** According to the Sepsis-3 definitions, sepsis is best defined as:

- A. Suspected infection plus ≥ 2 SIRS criteria
- B. Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection
- C. Infection plus serum lactate > 2 mmol/L
- D. Suspected infection plus qSOFA ≥ 2
- E. Infection requiring ICU admission

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** Sepsis-3 defines sepsis as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, operationalised as an acute SOFA increase of ≥ 2 points. The emphasis moved from inflammation (SIRS) to organ dysfunction, which carried ~10 % hospital mortality in the derivation cohorts.

**Why not the others:**

- A – Infection plus ≥ 2 SIRS criteria is the old Sepsis-1/2 concept; SIRS is both non-specific and insensitive.
- C – Lactate > 2 mmol/L is a criterion of septic shock (together with a vasopressor requirement), not of sepsis.
- D – qSOFA ≥ 2 is a prognostic prompt outside the ICU, not part of the definition.
- E – ICU admission depends on local resources and is unrelated to the definition.

**EDIC trap:** "SIRS" or "qSOFA" in a definition question is almost always the distractor.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. Sepsis-3. *JAMA* 2016;315:801–810.

</details>

**2.** Regarding septic shock according to Sepsis-3:

- A. The criteria apply despite adequate fluid resuscitation. — T / F
- B. Hospital mortality in the derivation cohorts was approximately 10 %. — T / F
- C. Serum lactate > 4 mmol/L is required. — T / F
- D. A vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg is required. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – T.**

- A (True) – Both clinical criteria must persist despite adequate volume resuscitation; hypotension from uncorrected hypovolaemia does not qualify.
- B (False) – Hospital mortality with both criteria exceeded **40 %**; ~10 % is the figure for sepsis (SOFA ≥ 2).
- C (False) – The threshold is lactate **> 2 mmol/L**; > 4 mmol/L belonged to the older EGDT/SSC era.
- D (True) – A vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg is the circulatory criterion and must coexist with lactate > 2 mmol/L.

**EDIC trap:** a vasopressor without hyperlactataemia, or hyperlactataemia alone, is not septic shock.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. Sepsis-3. *JAMA* 2016;315:801–810; Shankar-Hari M, et al. *JAMA* 2016;315:775–787.

</details>

**3.** Which of the following is NOT a component of qSOFA?

- A. Respiratory rate ≥ 22/min
- B. Systolic blood pressure ≤ 100 mmHg
- C. Altered mentation
- D. Heart rate > 90/min
- E. All of the above are qSOFA components

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** qSOFA has three items, 1 point each: respiratory rate ≥ 22/min, altered mentation (GCS < 15) and systolic BP ≤ 100 mmHg. Heart rate > 90/min is a SIRS criterion and has no place in qSOFA.

**Why not the others:**

- A – RR ≥ 22/min is a qSOFA item.
- B – SBP ≤ 100 mmHg is a qSOFA item.
- C – Altered mentation (any GCS < 15) is a qSOFA item.
- E – Incorrect, because heart rate is not a qSOFA component.

**EDIC trap:** mixing qSOFA with SIRS (HR > 90/min, RR > 20/min, temperature, WBC) or with the RR > 20 threshold.

**Ref:** Seymour CW, et al. *JAMA* 2016;315:762–774; Singer M, et al. *JAMA* 2016.

</details>

**4.** Concerning sepsis screening in the 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines:

- A. NEWS/NEWS2, MEWS or SIRS are recommended over qSOFA as a single screening tool. — T / F
- B. The recommendation regarding screening tools is conditional. — T / F
- C. Prehospital screening (e.g. in ambulances) is addressed by a strong recommendation. — T / F
- D. qSOFA is recommended as the single preferred screening tool in hospitalised patients. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – F.**

- A (True) – SSC 2026 recommends NEWS/NEWS2, MEWS or SIRS over qSOFA as a single screening tool in acutely ill hospitalised patients, because qSOFA has low sensitivity (good prognostic, poor screening tool).
- B (False) – It is a **strong** recommendation with moderate certainty of evidence.
- C (False) – Prehospital screening with a standard tool is new in 2026 but only **conditional, very low certainty**.
- D (False) – qSOFA is recommended **against** as a single screening tool.

**EDIC trap:** both SSC 2021 and SSC 2026 advised against qSOFA as a single screening tool.

**Ref:** Prescott HC, et al. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med* 2026 / *Crit Care Med* 2026;54:725–812.

</details>

**5.** A patient with pneumonia has a baseline SOFA of 0. Which acute change defines sepsis according to Sepsis-3?

- A. SOFA increase ≥ 2
- B. SOFA increase ≥ 3 in at least two organs
- C. SOFA increase ≥ 1
- D. Any SOFA increase with lactate > 2 mmol/L
- E. SOFA ≥ 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** Sepsis-3 operationalises organ dysfunction as an acute SOFA increase of ≥ 2 points consequent to infection. If pre-existing dysfunction is unknown, baseline SOFA is assumed to be 0, so a SOFA of ≥ 2 attributable to the pneumonia defines sepsis (~10 % hospital mortality).

**Why not the others:**

- B – There is no requirement for ≥ 3 points or for two organs; a total Δ ≥ 2 suffices.
- C – A 1-point rise is below the threshold.
- D – Lactate is not part of the sepsis definition; it is a septic shock criterion.
- E – The definition uses an acute change of ≥ 2, not an absolute value of 4.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. Sepsis-3. *JAMA* 2016;315:801–810.

</details>

**6.** Regarding the SOFA-2 score (JAMA 2025):

- A. It retains six organ systems. — T / F
- B. It was externally validated in more than one million patients. — T / F
- C. A gastrointestinal organ system was added. — T / F
- D. Its development combined a modified Delphi consensus with data-driven analysis. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – F, D – T.**

- A (True) – SOFA-2 keeps six organ systems, renamed brain, respiratory, cardiovascular, liver, kidney and hemostasis.
- B (True) – External validation in ~1.24 million ICU stays in 6 cohorts, after development and internal validation in ~2.1 million (≈ 3.3 million in total).
- C (False) – GI and immune dysfunction were discussed but **not included**, owing to a lack of reliable, widely available variables.
- D (True) – A modified Delphi consensus was combined with data-driven analysis; new variables reflect modern organ support (advanced respiratory support, mechanical circulatory support, RRT).

**EDIC trap:** expect principle questions (6 systems, what was not added) rather than exact thresholds.

**Ref:** Ranzani OT, et al. SOFA-2. *JAMA* 2025;334:2090–2103.

</details>

**7.** Which term was abandoned by the Sepsis-3 task force?

- A. Sepsis
- B. Severe sepsis
- C. Infection
- D. Septic shock
- E. Organ dysfunction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** The Sepsis-3 task force abandoned "severe sepsis" as redundant: once sepsis itself means infection-related organ dysfunction, a separate "severe" category adds nothing. Under Sepsis-1/2, sepsis = infection + SIRS and severe sepsis = sepsis + organ dysfunction.

**Why not the others:**

- A – Sepsis was redefined (dysregulated host response with organ dysfunction), not abandoned.
- C – Infection remains the prerequisite of sepsis.
- D – Septic shock was retained and redefined (vasopressor for MAP ≥ 65 mmHg + lactate > 2 mmol/L despite fluids).
- E – Organ dysfunction (Δ SOFA ≥ 2) is the core of the new definition.

**EDIC trap:** any option that uses "severe sepsis" as a current category is wrong.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. Sepsis-3. *JAMA* 2016;315:801–810.

</details>

**8.** Hyperlactataemia in sepsis:

- A. Is unaffected by hepatic lactate clearance. — T / F
- B. Is one of the two clinical criteria of septic shock. — T / F
- C. May result from β2-adrenergic stimulation of aerobic glycolysis. — T / F
- D. Always indicates tissue hypoxia. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (False) – Reduced hepatic lactate clearance (liver hypoperfusion or dysfunction) contributes to hyperlactataemia.
- B (True) – Lactate > 2 mmol/L together with a vasopressor requirement for MAP ≥ 65 mmHg despite adequate fluids defines septic shock.
- C (True) – Endogenous and infused catecholamines stimulate β2 receptors, accelerating aerobic glycolysis and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase activity, so lactate rises without tissue hypoxia.
- D (False) – Lactate also reflects adrenergic stimulation, reduced clearance and mitochondrial dysfunction; it is not a pure hypoxia marker, so fluids are not pushed merely to normalise it.

**EDIC trap:** "a high lactate always means hypoperfusion" – false.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. *JAMA* 2016; Garcia-Alvarez M, et al. *Crit Care* 2014;18:503; SSC 2026.

</details>

**9.** A 70-year-old with urosepsis is on noradrenaline 0.1 µg/kg/min for MAP 66 mmHg after 30 mL/kg crystalloid. Lactate is 1.4 mmol/L. According to Sepsis-3 he has:

- A. SIRS without sepsis
- B. Cannot be classified without qSOFA
- C. Septic shock
- D. Sepsis without septic shock
- E. Severe sepsis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** Septic shock requires **both** a vasopressor to keep MAP ≥ 65 mmHg **and** lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluids. With lactate 1.4 mmol/L only the circulatory criterion is met. He still has sepsis: noradrenaline ≤ 0.1 µg/kg/min scores 3 cardiovascular points in the original SOFA, so Δ SOFA ≥ 2.

**Why not the others:**

- A – Sepsis-3 no longer uses SIRS, and he has organ dysfunction due to infection.
- B – Classification uses SOFA; qSOFA is not needed.
- C – Lactate ≤ 2 mmol/L excludes septic shock by Sepsis-3.
- E – "Severe sepsis" was abandoned in 2016.

**EDIC trap:** hypotension on a vasopressor without hyperlactataemia is not septic shock.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. Sepsis-3. *JAMA* 2016;315:801–810.

</details>

**10.** Which statements about the original SOFA score are true?

- A. The neurological component uses the Glasgow Coma Scale. — T / F
- B. Each organ system scores 0–4 points. — T / F
- C. The renal component uses urine output or creatinine. — T / F
- D. The respiratory component uses the PaO2/FiO2 ratio. — T / F

**O1. (open)** A ward nurse calls you about a patient with suspected pneumonia who has qSOFA 2. Explain how you would classify this patient according to Sepsis-3, and why qSOFA alone is insufficient both to diagnose sepsis and to screen for it.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

**O2. (open)** Explain why the original SOFA score needed updating and summarise the main features of SOFA-2.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Original SOFA (1996): 6 systems, 0–4 points each, max 24.
- Weaknesses: outdated vasopressor doses (dopamine as reference), no account of modern organ support (HFNO, ECMO, RRT, mechanical circulatory support), difficulty assessing GCS in sedated patients.
- SOFA-2 (JAMA 2025) developed by modified Delphi consensus plus data-driven analysis.
- Development/internal validation in ~2.1 million and external validation in ~1.2 million patients from diverse settings.
- Retains 6 systems, renamed: brain, respiratory, cardiovascular, liver, kidney, hemostasis; new variables and thresholds reflecting current organ support.
- GI and immune dysfunction were not included (lack of reliable, widely available variables).
- The sepsis definition (Δ ≥ 2 points) remains tied to the Sepsis-3 concept.

</details>


## W01 · D2 – SSC 2026: screening, lactate, timing of antibiotics

**1.** According to SSC 2026, an adult with probable bacterial pneumonia and sepsis, but NOT in shock, should receive antimicrobials:

- A. Within 6 hours, after results of procalcitonin
- B. Within 24 hours
- C. Within 3 hours of recognition
- D. Only after blood cultures result
- E. Immediately, ideally within 1 hour of recognition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** For **probable or definite** sepsis without shock SSC 2026 recommends antimicrobials immediately, ideally within 1 h of recognition (strong, very low certainty) – the same window as for septic shock. The 3-h window is reserved for *possible* sepsis without shock after a time-limited rapid assessment.

**Why not the others:**

- A – Procalcitonin is not used to decide whether to start antibiotics, and 6 h is not an SSC window.
- B – 24 h is far beyond any SSC time frame.
- C – Within 3 h applies to *possible*, not probable, sepsis without shock.
- D – Cultures are taken before antibiotics only if this causes no substantial delay; results are never awaited.

**EDIC trap:** "possible" (3 h) vs "probable/definite" (1 h) is the key word in the stem.

**Ref:** Prescott HC, et al. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med* 2026 / *Crit Care Med* 2026;54:725–812.

</details>

**2.** In the GRADE system as used by SSC:

- A. Best-practice statements are ungraded. — T / F
- B. "We recommend" denotes a strong recommendation. — T / F
- C. "We suggest" denotes a conditional recommendation. — T / F
- D. A strong recommendation can be made even when the certainty of evidence is very low. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – T.**

- A (True) – Best practice statements are ungraded; they are used when benefit is unequivocal (e.g. "sepsis is a medical emergency").
- B (True) – "We recommend" = strong: most informed clinicians would act this way.
- C (True) – "We suggest" = conditional (weak): reasonable variation according to patient and context.
- D (True) – GRADE separates strength from certainty; a strong recommendation can rest on very low certainty when benefit clearly outweighs harm, e.g. antibiotics within 1 h in septic shock.

**EDIC trap:** "a strong recommendation requires high-certainty evidence" – false.

**Ref:** Prescott HC, et al. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med* 2026 / *Crit Care Med* 2026;54:725–812. Guyatt GH, et al. GRADE. *BMJ* 2008;336:924–926.

</details>

**3.** A patient has possible sepsis without shock and the likelihood of infection is judged low. SSC 2026 suggests:

- A. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
- B. Procalcitonin-guided initiation of antibiotics
- C. Empirical antifungal therapy
- D. Deferring antimicrobials while closely monitoring the patient
- E. Antibiotics within 3 hours regardless of further findings

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** New in SSC 2026: in possible sepsis without shock and with a **low likelihood of infection**, deferring antimicrobials while closely monitoring the patient is suggested (conditional, very low certainty). This reflects stewardship – unnecessary antibiotics cause resistance, C. difficile infection and toxicity, and may delay an alternative diagnosis.

**Why not the others:**

- A – The 1-h window is for shock or probable/definite sepsis.
- B – Procalcitonin should not be used to decide whether to start antibiotics.
- C – Empirical antifungals have no role when infection itself is unlikely.
- E – The 3-h window applies only if concern for infection persists after rapid assessment, not "regardless".

**Ref:** Prescott HC, et al. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med* 2026 / *Crit Care Med* 2026;54:725–812.

</details>

**4.** Regarding lactate in SSC 2026:

- A. Measuring lactate is suggested only in patients with septic shock. — T / F
- B. Capillary refill time may be used as an adjunct to other perfusion measures. — T / F
- C. Serial lactate measurements are suggested to guide resuscitation when lactate is elevated. — T / F
- D. Fluids should be continued until lactate normalises. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**9.** Which statement best explains why SSC distinguishes antibiotic timing between shock and sepsis without shock?

- A. The association between delay and mortality is strongest in shock, while over-treatment of non-infected patients carries harm
- B. Antibiotics are ineffective in shock
- C. Randomised trials proved harm of early antibiotics in sepsis without shock
- D. Patients without shock never have bacteraemia
- E. Blood cultures are only positive in shock

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** Observational data show the strongest association between antibiotic delay and mortality in **septic shock**, whereas in sepsis without shock the association is weaker and over-treating non-infected patients causes harm (resistance, C. difficile, toxicity, missed alternative diagnoses). Hence 1 h in shock and a time-limited assessment (up to 3 h) in possible sepsis without shock.

**Why not the others:**

- B – Antibiotics are lifesaving in shock; that is why they are given fastest.
- C – No RCT has shown harm from early antibiotics; the evidence is mainly observational.
- D – Bacteraemia also occurs in patients without shock.
- E – Blood cultures are positive in a minority overall, with or without shock.

**Ref:** Prescott HC, et al. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med* 2026 / *Crit Care Med* 2026;54:725–812. Seymour CW, et al. *N Engl J Med* 2017;376:2235–2244.

**13.** The main reason dopamine is not recommended as first-line vasopressor is:

- A. It is only available as an oral drug
- B. It is ineffective at raising MAP
- C. Higher arrhythmia rate and worse outcomes in cardiogenic shock (SOAP II)
- D. It causes renal failure in all patients
- E. It causes severe hyponatraemia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** SOAP II showed about twice as many arrhythmic events with dopamine (24 % vs 12 %) and higher mortality in the cardiogenic shock subgroup, with no advantage elsewhere. **Why not the others:**

- A – Dopamine is an IV drug.

- D – It does not cause renal failure; it simply offers no renal protection.

**15.** Which statement about balanced crystalloids vs saline is most accurate?

- A. All large RCTs (SMART, BaSICS, PLUS) showed significant mortality reduction with balanced solutions
- B. PLUS showed harm with Plasma-Lyte
- C. SMART showed a reduction in the composite MAKE30; BaSICS and PLUS showed no significant mortality difference
- D. Saline is preferred in all septic patients
- E. Balanced solutions are contraindicated in hyperkalaemia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** SMART (15,802 adults) showed a lower composite MAKE30 with balanced crystalloids (14.3 % vs 15.4 %), whereas BaSICS and PLUS showed no significant 90-day mortality difference. This is why SSC suggests balanced over saline only conditionally (moderate certainty).

**Why not the others:**

- A – Only SMART was positive, and only for a composite outcome.
- B – PLUS showed no harm (21.8 % vs 22.0 %).
- D – Saline is preferred only in TBI.
- E – A myth: balanced solutions contain K⁺ (~4–5 mmol/L), but saline causes acidosis that shifts K⁺ out of cells, so balanced fluids do not worsen hyperkalaemia more than saline.

**Ref:** Semler MW, et al. *N Engl J Med* 2018; Zampieri FG, et al. *JAMA* 2021; Finfer S, et al. *N Engl J Med* 2022.
