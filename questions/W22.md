## W22 · D1 – Red cell transfusion thresholds: TRICC to TRICS-III, MINT and TRAIN

**1.** A 62-year-old man is on day 4 in ICU with septic shock from pneumonia, now on noradrenaline 0.08 µg/kg/min. Lactate is 1.6 mmol/L, Hb 7.6 g/dL and there is no bleeding. What is the most appropriate transfusion approach?

- A. Transfuse 2 units to reach Hb ≥ 10 g/dL
- B. Transfuse 1 unit to keep haematocrit ≥ 30 %
- C. Do not transfuse now; transfuse if Hb falls below 7 g/dL
- D. Transfuse if ScvO₂ is below 70 %
- E. Start erythropoietin and IV iron instead of transfusion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** In septic shock, TRISS (n = 1,005) found no difference in 90-day mortality between thresholds of 7 and 9 g/dL (43.0 vs 45.0 %), with half the units transfused. ESICM 2020 suggests a restrictive threshold of 7 g/dL in sepsis.

**Why not the others:**
- A – liberal targets bring no benefit in sepsis and expose the patient to TACO and other risks.
- B – the haematocrit ≥ 30 % target came from the Rivers EGDT protocol and has been abandoned.
- D – ESICM 2020 recommends Hb triggers over alternative physiological triggers.
- E – ESICM 2020 suggests against EPO and/or iron to prevent transfusion in non-bleeding ICU patients.

**Ref:** Holst LB, N Engl J Med 2014; Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**2.** Regarding the TRICC trial (general ICU, Hb thresholds 7 vs 10 g/dL):

- A. 30-day mortality was significantly lower with the restrictive strategy in the whole population. — T / F
- B. Patients with APACHE II ≤ 20 had lower mortality with the restrictive strategy. — T / F
- C. The restrictive strategy reduced the number of units transfused. — T / F
- D. The trial excluded all patients with cardiovascular disease. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (False) – 18.7 vs 23.3 %, p = 0.11; the primary outcome was not significant.
- B (True) – benefit was seen in less sick (APACHE II ≤ 20) and younger (< 55 years) patients.
- C (True) – fewer units, and one third of restrictive patients avoided transfusion.
- D (False) – patients with cardiovascular disease were included; a subgroup analysis found no clear harm, although severe ischaemic heart disease was under-represented.

**Ref:** Hébert PC, N Engl J Med 1999.

</details>

**3.** A 71-year-old woman is admitted to the coronary care unit with a type 1 NSTEMI. She is haemodynamically stable, with no bleeding, and her Hb is 8.4 g/dL. Based on the MINT trial, which approach is best supported?

- A. No transfusion unless Hb falls below 7 g/dL
- B. No transfusion unless she develops shock
- C. Transfuse only if troponin keeps rising
- D. Transfusion to maintain Hb at about 10 g/dL
- E. Transfuse to Hb ≥ 12 g/dL

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** In MINT (n = 3,504, MI with Hb < 10 g/dL), death or re-infarction at 30 days was 16.9 % with a restrictive (7–8 g/dL) vs 14.5 % with a liberal (≥ 10 g/dL) strategy (RR 1.15, 95 % CI 0.99–1.34). The result was not significant, but the restrictive strategy could not be shown to be safe, and all components favoured liberal. ESICM 2020 suggests 9–10 g/dL in ACS.

**Why not the others:**
- A – this is the general ICU threshold; it is not supported in acute MI.
- B – waiting for shock ignores ischaemic risk.
- C – troponin is not a validated transfusion trigger.
- E – no trial supports supranormal Hb, and it increases volume overload.

**Ref:** Carson JL, N Engl J Med 2023; Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**4.** A 68-year-old man is 6 hours after CABG and aortic valve replacement (EuroSCORE II 7 %). Chest drain loss is minimal, he is haemodynamically stable and his Hb is 8.0 g/dL. What is the most appropriate threshold for red cell transfusion?

- A. 7.5 g/dL
- B. 9.5 g/dL
- C. 10 g/dL
- D. 6 g/dL
- E. Haematocrit 35 %

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** TRICS-III (n = 5,243 moderate-to-high-risk cardiac surgical patients) showed that a restrictive threshold of 7.5 g/dL was non-inferior to 9.5 g/dL for death, MI, stroke or new dialysis (11.4 vs 12.5 %). ESICM 2020 strongly recommends 7.5 g/dL after cardiac surgery.

**Why not the others:**
- B – this was the liberal arm, with more transfusion and no benefit.
- C – no evidence supports 10 g/dL after cardiac surgery without bleeding or ischaemia.
- D – this is below all tested thresholds.
- E – haematocrit targets of 30–35 % are obsolete.

**Ref:** Mazer CD, N Engl J Med 2017; Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**5.** Regarding red cell transfusion in acute brain injury:

- A. In TRAIN, a liberal threshold of 9 g/dL was associated with fewer unfavourable neurological outcomes at 180 days than 7 g/dL. — T / F
- B. HEMOTION (TBI) showed a significant improvement in 6-month functional outcome with a threshold of 10 g/dL. — T / F
- C. The ESICM 2020 guideline makes a strong recommendation for a threshold of 7 g/dL in traumatic brain injury. — T / F
- D. In SAHaRA (aneurysmal SAH), a threshold of 10 g/dL significantly reduced unfavourable outcome at 12 months. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – F.**

- B (False) – 68.4 vs 73.5 % unfavourable GOS-E; not significant, though in the same direction.
- C (False) – ESICM 2020 made no recommendation (either 7 or 9–11.5 g/dL acceptable).

**Ref:** Taccone FS, JAMA 2024; Turgeon AF, N Engl J Med 2024; Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**6.** A 55-year-old man with alcohol-related cirrhosis presents with haematemesis from oesophageal varices. After terlipressin, antibiotics and banding he is stable, with Hb 7.4 g/dL. What is the most appropriate threshold for red cell transfusion?

- A. 10 g/dL to protect the liver
- B. 9 g/dL to prevent rebleeding
- C. 8 g/dL with 2 units given at once
- D. 7 g/dL, targeting about 7–9 g/dL
- E. Transfuse according to INR

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** In Villanueva's trial (n = 921, upper GI bleeding), a threshold of 7 g/dL reduced 6-week mortality (5 vs 9 %) and rebleeding compared with 9 g/dL. In variceal bleeding, over-transfusion raises portal pressure and promotes rebleeding.

**Why not the others:**
- A – liberal transfusion increased portal pressure and mortality in cirrhosis.
- B – 9 g/dL was the liberal arm, with worse outcomes.
- C – single-unit transfusion and reassessment is preferred.
- E – INR does not reflect bleeding risk in cirrhosis and does not guide red cell transfusion.

**Ref:** Villanueva C, N Engl J Med 2013; Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**7.** Regarding platelet and plasma transfusion in non-bleeding critically ill adults:

- A. Prophylactic platelet transfusion is suggested only when the count is below 10 × 10⁹/L. — T / F
- B. In PACER, withholding platelets before ultrasound-guided CVC insertion at counts of 10–50 × 10⁹/L was non-inferior for bleeding. — T / F
- C. ESICM 2020 suggests against prophylactic plasma before bedside invasive procedures in coagulopathic patients. — T / F
- D. A platelet count of 70 × 10⁹/L is an indication for platelets before percutaneous tracheostomy. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – conditional recommendation, very low certainty.
- B (False) – grade 2–4 bleeding occurred in 11.9 % without vs 4.8 % with transfusion; non-inferiority was not shown.
- C (True) – INR poorly predicts procedural bleeding, and FFP carries TACO/TRALI risk.
- D (False) – ESICM suggests against platelets before CVC or percutaneous tracheostomy at 50–100 × 10⁹/L.

**Ref:** Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020; van Baarle FLF, N Engl J Med 2023.

</details>

**8.** A 79-year-old woman with heart failure receives 2 units of red cells over 2 hours for Hb 6.8 g/dL. Three hours later she is dyspnoeic, BP 172/94 mmHg, with raised JVP, bilateral crackles and SpO₂ 86 %. What is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Transfusion-related acute lung injury
- B. Acute haemolytic reaction
- C. Anaphylaxis
- D. Febrile non-haemolytic reaction
- E. Transfusion-associated circulatory overload

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** TACO occurs within 12 hours of transfusion, typically in elderly patients with cardiac or renal disease and fast or large-volume transfusion. It presents with hypertension, raised JVP, a positive fluid balance and a rise in BNP, and it responds to diuretics. It is the leading cause of transfusion-related death in haemovigilance reports.

**Why not the others:**
- A – TRALI typically causes hypotension and fever, with no left atrial hypertension, and does not respond to diuretics.
- B – haemolysis presents with fever, loin pain, hypotension and haemoglobinuria.
- C – anaphylaxis occurs within minutes, with hypotension, bronchospasm and urticaria.
- D – a febrile reaction causes fever without hypoxaemia.

**Ref:** Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020; Carson JL, JAMA 2023.

</details>

**9.** All of the following are recommended or suggested by the ESICM 2020 guideline for non-bleeding critically ill adults EXCEPT:

- A. A restrictive Hb threshold of 7 g/dL in general ICU patients
- B. Intravenous iron to reduce red cell transfusion
- C. Small-volume blood sampling tubes
- D. A threshold of 7.5 g/dL after cardiac surgery
- E. Haemoglobin rather than physiological triggers

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** ESICM 2020 suggests against iron (oral or IV), erythropoietin, or both, to prevent transfusion in non-bleeding ICU patients (conditional, low certainty). Inflammation-driven hepcidin limits iron use, and trials showed little reduction in transfusion and no outcome benefit.

**Why not the others:**
- A – strong recommendation, moderate certainty.
- C – a conditional recommendation, as phlebotomy is a major cause of ICU anaemia.
- D – strong recommendation based on TRICS-III.
- E – conditional recommendation; ScvO₂ and lactate are not validated triggers.

**Ref:** Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

- B. Trials such as ABLE and TRANSFUSE showed lower mortality with fresher red cells. — T / F
- C. The myocardium has a high resting oxygen extraction, so increased supply depends mainly on increased coronary flow. — T / F

**11.** A 34-year-old woman has severe TBI (GCS 6) after a road traffic collision. On day 2 she is not bleeding, ICP is controlled and Hb is 8.1 g/dL. Which statement best reflects current evidence?

- A. A strong guideline recommendation mandates 7 g/dL
- B. TRAIN suggests that a threshold of about 9 g/dL may improve functional outcome, although HEMOTION was neutral
- C. Transfusion should target Hb ≥ 12 g/dL to maximise cerebral oxygen delivery
- D. Erythropoietin should replace transfusion
- E. Transfusion is contraindicated because it raises ICP

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** TRAIN (TBI, SAH and ICH) found fewer unfavourable outcomes with 9 vs 7 g/dL (62.6 vs 72.6 %). HEMOTION (TBI, 10 vs 7 g/dL) and SAHaRA (SAH) were neutral but in the same direction. No guideline yet gives a firm threshold, but many neurointensivists now aim for about 9 g/dL.

**Why not the others:**
- A – ESICM 2020 made no recommendation for acute brain injury.
- C – no trial supports Hb ≥ 12 g/dL.
- D – erythropoietin is not a substitute and increases thrombosis (EPO-3).
- E – transfusion does not raise ICP; anaemia may worsen cerebral hypoxia.

**Ref:** Taccone FS, JAMA 2024; Turgeon AF, N Engl J Med 2024.

</details>

**O1. (open)** Summarise the evidence-based red cell transfusion thresholds for different groups of ICU patients, naming the key trial behind each.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:

**O2. (open)** A patient develops hypoxaemia during a transfusion. How do you distinguish TRALI from TACO?

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Both cause acute hypoxaemia with bilateral infiltrates within hours of transfusion (TRALI ≤ 6 h, TACO ≤ 12 h).
- TRALI: hypotension, fever, normal LA pressure/JVP, echo without LV dysfunction, BNP normal or mildly raised, exudative oedema fluid, often caused by donor anti-HLA/HNA antibodies; treat supportively (lung-protective ventilation), avoid diuretics.
- TACO: hypertension, raised JVP, positive balance, LV dysfunction, BNP/NT-proBNP raised, responds to diuretics; risk factors are elderly patients, heart or kidney failure, and fast transfusion.
- In both: stop the transfusion, check product identity, report to haemovigilance; prevention – restrictive thresholds, single units, slow rates, male-donor plasma (TRALI).

</details>


## W22 · D2 – DIC and coagulopathy in the ICU: scores, anticoagulant reversal, acquired bleeding disorders

**1.** A 64-year-old woman with faecal peritonitis is on day 2 in ICU. Platelets 42 × 10⁹/L, PT prolonged by 4 s, D-dimer strongly increased, fibrinogen 2.8 g/L. There is no bleeding. What is her ISTH overt DIC score, and what does it mean?

- A. 4 – non-overt DIC
- B. 7 – overt DIC
- C. 6 – overt DIC
- D. 5 – overt DIC
- E. 3 – no DIC

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** Platelets < 50 × 10⁹/L = 2, a strongly increased fibrin marker = 3, PT prolonged by 3–6 s = 1, fibrinogen > 1 g/L = 0. The total of 6 is ≥ 5, which means overt DIC, in a patient with a compatible underlying disorder (sepsis).

**Why not the others:**
- A – this miscounts the D-dimer (a strong increase scores 3, not 2).
- B – this wrongly scores PT as 2 (that needs > 6 s prolongation).
- D – this wrongly scores the PT as 0.
- E – the score is well above the threshold.

**Ref:** Taylor FB, Thromb Haemost 2001.

</details>

**2.** Regarding sepsis-associated DIC:

- A. Fibrinolysis is typically suppressed because of high PAI-1 levels. — T / F
- B. Fibrinogen is often normal because it is an acute-phase protein. — T / F
- C. Tranexamic acid is recommended to limit microvascular bleeding. — T / F
- D. The SIC score uses platelet count, PT-INR and SOFA score. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – F, D – T.**

- A (True) – septic DIC is the thrombotic, organ-failure phenotype with fibrinolytic shutdown.
- B (True) – low fibrinogen is more typical of hyperfibrinolytic DIC (APL, obstetric, trauma).
- C (False) – antifibrinolytics are contraindicated in septic DIC, because they promote microthrombosis.
- D (True) – SIC ≥ 4 points (with platelets + INR > 2).

**Ref:** Iba T, J Thromb Haemost 2025; Iba T, BMJ Open 2017.

</details>

**3.** A 76-year-old man on warfarin for atrial fibrillation presents with a spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage. INR is 3.4, GCS 13. What is the most appropriate reversal?

- A. Fresh frozen plasma 15 mL/kg alone
- B. Vitamin K 10 mg orally
- C. Recombinant activated factor VII
- D. Protamine 50 mg IV
- E. Four-factor PCC 25–50 IU/kg plus vitamin K 5–10 mg IV

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** 4F-PCC corrects the INR within minutes, with a small volume and better haemostatic efficacy than FFP. IV vitamin K is required because the effect of PCC wanes after 6–8 h, while vitamin K restores endogenous synthesis within 6–12 h.

**Why not the others:**
- A – FFP needs large volumes, takes time to thaw, and corrects the INR slowly and incompletely.
- B – oral vitamin K is too slow for life-threatening bleeding.
- C – rFVIIa is not recommended for VKA reversal (thrombosis, unreliable reversal).
- D – protamine reverses heparin, not warfarin.

**Ref:** Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2021.

</details>

**4.** A 70-year-old woman on apixaban 5 mg twice daily (last dose 4 hours ago) has an acute intracerebral haemorrhage with GCS 14. Regarding andexanet alfa compared with usual care (mostly PCC) in ANNEXA-I, which statement is correct?

- A. It reduced 30-day mortality

- C – haematoma expansion was the main component of the haemostatic efficacy benefit.

- E – protamine has no role in reversing factor Xa inhibitors.

**Ref:** Connolly SJ, N Engl J Med 2024.

</details>

**6.** A 72-year-old woman develops large spontaneous haematomas of the thigh and flank. Platelets 260 × 10⁹/L, PT normal, aPTT 78 s, not corrected after incubation with normal plasma. She takes no anticoagulants. What is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Acquired haemophilia A (FVIII inhibitor)
- B. Vitamin K deficiency
- C. Lupus anticoagulant
- D. Factor XII deficiency
- E. Disseminated intravascular coagulation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** An isolated prolonged aPTT that is not corrected by mixing (an inhibitor), together with severe soft-tissue bleeding in an older patient, is typical of acquired haemophilia A. Confirm with a low FVIII level and Bethesda titre; treat with bypassing agents (rFVIIa, aPCC) or emicizumab and immunosuppression.

**Why not the others:**
- B – this would prolong the PT first and would correct on mixing.
- C – lupus anticoagulant does not correct on mixing either, but it causes thrombosis, not bleeding.
- D – FXII deficiency prolongs the aPTT without bleeding and corrects on mixing.
- E – DIC would prolong the PT and lower platelets.

**Ref:** Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2021.

**7.** Regarding coagulation in patients with cirrhosis:

- B. Levels of both procoagulant and anticoagulant factors are reduced. — T / F

**8.** A 38-year-old man is admitted with gum bleeding, ecchymoses and fatigue. Blast cells with Auer rods are seen on the blood film. Platelets 18 × 10⁹/L, fibrinogen 0.7 g/L, PT prolonged by 7 s, D-dimer very high. What is the most important immediate treatment, in addition to blood products?

**Why not the others:**

**9.** A 58-year-old man with upper GI bleeding from a duodenal ulcer is haemodynamically stable after endoscopic therapy. A colleague suggests a high-dose tranexamic acid infusion. Which statement about the evidence is correct?

- A. HALT-IT showed a significant reduction in death from bleeding
- B. HALT-IT showed no reduction in death from bleeding and more venous thromboembolic events
- C. Tranexamic acid is contraindicated in all bleeding patients
- D. Tranexamic acid increases arterial events only
- E. Tranexamic acid is effective only when given orally

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Why not the others:**
- A – the trial was neutral for its primary outcome.
- C – TXA is beneficial in trauma (CRASH-2) and postpartum haemorrhage (WOMAN).
- D – the excess was in venous events, not arterial events.
- E – HALT-IT used IV TXA; the route was not the issue.

**Ref:** HALT-IT Collaborators, Lancet 2020.

**11.** A 45-year-old woman with sepsis and overt DIC (ISTH score 6) is not bleeding. Platelets are 38 × 10⁹/L and fibrinogen 2.1 g/L. Which of the following is LEAST appropriate?

- A. Treating the underlying infection, with source control
- B. Prophylactic platelet transfusion to reach > 100 × 10⁹/L
- C. Daily reassessment of the DIC score
- D. Pharmacological VTE prophylaxis, adjusted to platelet count and bleeding risk
- E. Platelet transfusion before a planned invasive procedure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** Blood products in DIC are given for bleeding or before procedures, not to normalise laboratory values. In a non-bleeding patient, prophylactic platelets are given only below about 10–20 × 10⁹/L. Transfusing to > 100 × 10⁹/L has no benefit and may fuel thrombosis.

**Why not the others:**
- A – treating the cause is the cornerstone of DIC therapy.
- C – the score is dynamic and should be repeated daily.
- D – non-bleeding DIC patients have a high VTE risk; prophylaxis is often continued or adjusted when platelets fall below about 30–50 × 10⁹/L.
- E – platelets (target > 50 × 10⁹/L) are appropriate before invasive procedures.

**Ref:** Iba T, J Thromb Haemost 2025; Vlaar AP, Intensive Care Med 2021.

**O1. (open)** Describe the pathophysiological differences between septic DIC and the DIC of acute promyelocytic leukaemia and how they influence treatment.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Septic DIC: TF expression on monocytes/endothelium, NETs, loss of natural anticoagulants (antithrombin, protein C, thrombomodulin), high PAI-1 → suppressed fibrinolysis, microthrombosis, organ failure; bleeding uncommon; fibrinogen often normal.

- Scores: ISTH overt DIC (≥ 5) and SIC (≥ 4); ISTH 2025 update – early (disease-specific) and late phases.

**Why C:** In CRASH-2 the benefit of TXA was greatest within 1 hour and present up to 3 hours. Given after 3 hours it increased bleeding deaths, probably because coagulopathy shifts toward fibrinolytic shutdown. The European guideline recommends TXA within 3 h. Later use is reserved for documented hyperfibrinolysis (e.g. LY30 > 3 %, ML > 15 %).

**Why not the others:**
- A – the dose is not increased.
- B – effectiveness decreases with time.
- D – TXA is not guided by fibrinogen level.
- E – CRASH-2 showed no increase in vascular occlusive events.

**Ref:** CRASH-2 collaborators, Lancet 2010; Rossaint R, Crit Care 2023. → [D5](D5.md)

**14.** A 60-year-old man is bleeding after a liver resection. ROTEM shows EXTEM CT 95 s (prolonged), EXTEM A5 36 mm (normal), FIBTEM A5 12 mm (normal) and EXTEM ML 6 %. INTEM and HEPTEM CT are both equally prolonged. Which of the following are appropriate interpretations?

- A. The pattern suggests deficiency of coagulation factors. — T / F
- B. Fibrinogen concentrate is the priority. — T / F
- C. There is evidence of hyperfibrinolysis requiring TXA. — T / F
- D. Protamine is indicated. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – F.**

- A (True) – a prolonged CT with normal clot firmness suggests a factor deficit, which can be treated with plasma or PCC.
- B (False) – FIBTEM A5 12 mm is adequate.
- C (False) – ML 6 % is below the 15 % threshold.
- D (False) – INTEM and HEPTEM are equally prolonged, so there is no heparin effect.

**Ref:** Rossaint R, Crit Care 2023; Kietaibl S, Eur J Anaesthesiol 2023. → [D5](D5.md)

</details>

**15.** Regarding massive transfusion in trauma:

- A. Permissive hypotension with SBP 80–90 mmHg is recommended in patients with severe TBI. — T / F
- B. Ionised calcium should be kept within the normal range during massive transfusion. — T / F
- C. Platelets should be kept above 100 × 10⁹/L in ongoing bleeding with TBI. — T / F
- D. In PROPPR, a 1:1:1 ratio significantly reduced 30-day mortality. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (False) – MAP ≥ 80 mmHg is recommended in severe TBI; permissive hypotension applies only without TBI.
- B (True) – citrate causes hypocalcaemia, which impairs coagulation and cardiac function.
- C (True) – the European guideline 2023 (2C).
- D (False) – 22.4 vs 26.1 %, NS; it improved haemostasis and reduced exsanguination at 24 h.

**Ref:** Rossaint R, Crit Care 2023; Holcomb JB, JAMA 2015. → [D5](D5.md)

</details>
