## W02 · D1 – Antibiotic PK/PD in the critically ill and prolonged β-lactam infusion

**1.** A 24-year-old man with polytrauma develops ventilator-associated pneumonia on day 6. Serum creatinine is 55 µmol/L. Measured 8-h urinary creatinine clearance is 190 mL/min/1.73 m². Which statement is most accurate?

- A. He has augmented renal clearance and is at risk of subtherapeutic β-lactam concentrations
- B. Aminoglycoside trough monitoring is unnecessary in this patient
- C. Standard meropenem dosing will reliably achieve target concentrations because renal function is normal
- D. The loading dose of meropenem should be reduced
- E. The Cockcroft-Gault equation is the best method to detect this condition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** ARC = CrCl > 130 mL/min/1.73 m² from a measured urine collection; a young polytrauma patient is typical. Estimating equations do not detect ARC; the loading dose is not reduced.

</details>

**2.** Regarding pharmacokinetic changes in septic shock:

- A. The volume of distribution of hydrophilic antibiotics is typically increased. — T / F
- B. The loading dose of a β-lactam should be reduced in acute kidney injury. — T / F
- C. Aminoglycosides and β-lactams are lipophilic antibiotics. — T / F
- D. Hypoalbuminaemia may increase the clearance of highly protein-bound drugs such as ceftriaxone. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – T.** B – the loading dose depends on Vd, not on renal function. C – both are hydrophilic (low Vd, renal elimination); fluoroquinolones and macrolides are lipophilic.

</details>

**3.** The pharmacodynamic index that best predicts the efficacy of meropenem is:

- A. Post-antibiotic effect duration
- B. Trough concentration relative to the toxic threshold
- C. Cmax/MIC
- D. AUC₀₋₂₄/MIC
- E. Percentage of the dosing interval during which the free concentration exceeds the MIC (% fT>MIC)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.** β-lactams are time-dependent.

</details>

**4.** Regarding the BLING III trial:

- A. It enrolled fewer than 1000 critically ill adults with sepsis. — T / F
- B. Piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem was given by continuous or intermittent infusion. — T / F
- C. 90-day all-cause mortality was significantly lower with continuous infusion. — T / F
- D. Clinical cure was similar in both groups. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – F, D – F.** A – 7031 patients. C – 24.9 vs 26.8 %, p = 0.08. D – clinical cure was higher with continuous infusion (55.7 vs 50.0 %).

</details>

**5.** According to the 2020 ESICM/ESCMID-endorsed Position Paper on antimicrobial TDM, routine TDM in critically ill patients is recommended for:

**D.** 

</details>

**8.** Regarding aminoglycoside dosing in the ICU:

- A. Dosing in three divided daily doses is preferred to once-daily administration. — T / F
- B. Efficacy is concentration-dependent. — T / F
- C. A high trough concentration is the target for efficacy. — T / F

**9.** In BLING II (AJRCCM 2015), continuous vs intermittent β-lactam infusion in 432 patients with severe sepsis resulted in:

- A. Higher rates of Clostridioides difficile infection with continuous infusion
- B. A significant reduction in 90-day mortality

**C.** 18 vs 20 days, p = 0.38.

</details>

**10.** When starting a prolonged meropenem infusion in septic shock:

- A. SSC 2026 recommends prolonged infusion for maintenance with a strong recommendation. — T / F
- B. Drug stability at room temperature should be considered. — T / F
- C. An initial loading bolus should precede the infusion. — T / F
- D. Prolonged infusion makes TDM unnecessary in patients on ECMO. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.** A – strong, moderate certainty (upgraded from weak in SSC 2021).

</details>

**O1. (open)** A 24-year-old with polytrauma and VAP is started on meropenem. Outline how you would plan his antibiotic dosing, explaining the pharmacokinetic changes that put him at risk of underdosing.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- ↑ Vd of hydrophilic drugs (capillary leak, fluids, oedema, drains) → full loading dose, independent of renal function.
- Suspect ARC (young polytrauma): CrCl > 130 mL/min/1.73 m² on an 8–24 h urine collection; Cockcroft-Gault and normal creatinine do not exclude it.
- Meropenem is time-dependent (% fT>MIC); in the critically ill aim for 100 % fT>MIC.
- After the bolus, prolonged/continuous infusion (SSC 2026: strong, moderate certainty); consider room-temperature stability and line compatibility.
- With ARC use higher/more frequent doses or continuous infusion.
- β-lactam TDM (trough 24–48 h after start, or any time at steady state with continuous infusion), especially useful in ARC, obesity, RRT/ECMO, high-MIC pathogens.
- Consider hypoalbuminaemia if a highly protein-bound drug is used.

</details>

**O2. (open)** Explain why BLING III was "negative" while the meta-analysis published alongside it was "positive", and how you would interpret the evidence for prolonged β-lactam infusion.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- BLING III: 7031 patients, 104 ICUs, pip-tazo or meropenem, open-label; 90-d mortality 24.9 vs 26.8 %, OR 0.91 (95 % CI 0.81–1.01), p = 0.08 – NS.
- Its CI includes a clinically important benefit; clinical cure was higher (55.7 vs 50.0 %); no difference in MDR colonisation/infection or C. difficile.
- The meta-analysis (18 RCTs, 9108 patients, Bayesian) included BLING III and gained precision.
- Result: 90-d mortality RR 0.86 (95 % CrI 0.72–0.98), 99.1 % posterior probability of benefit, high certainty; ↑ clinical cure (RR 1.16), ↓ ICU mortality.
- BLING II (432 patients) showed no difference in ICU-free days (18 vs 20, p = 0.38) – underpowered.
- Interpretation: strongest evidence to date that prolonged β-lactam infusion (after a loading bolus) reduces mortality in sepsis.

</details>


## W02 · D2 – De-escalation, duration of therapy and procalcitonin

**1.** A 60-year-old with E. coli bacteraemia from pyelonephritis is afebrile and haemodynamically stable on day 5 of appropriate therapy, with no abscess on imaging. According to the BALANCE trial, the most appropriate total antibiotic duration is:

- A. 7 days
- B. 14 days
- C. Until procalcitonin is undetectable
- D. 21 days
- E. 3 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** BALANCE: 7 days non-inferior to 14 days for 90-d mortality.

</details>

**2.** Regarding the PRORATA trial:

- A. 28-day mortality was significantly lower in the PCT group. — T / F
- B. The PCT group had more antibiotic-free days by day 28. — T / F
- C. Antibiotics were discontinued only when PCT fell below 0.1 µg/L. — T / F
- D. Relapse of infection was significantly more frequent in the PCT group. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – F, D – F.** B – 14.3 vs 11.6. A – 21.2 vs 20.4 %, non-inferior. C – the stopping rule was PCT < 0.5 µg/L or a decrease ≥ 80 % from peak. D – 6.5 vs 5.1 %, p = 0.45.

</details>

**3.** In ADAPT-Sepsis (JAMA 2025), which strategy reduced total antibiotic duration while being non-inferior for 28-day mortality?

- A. Daily CRP-guided protocol
- B. Fixed 5-day course
- C. Both CRP and procalcitonin protocols
- D. Neither protocol
- E. Daily procalcitonin-guided protocol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.** CRP did not shorten therapy and non-inferiority for mortality was not demonstrated.

</details>

**4.** Regarding the PneumA trial of 8 vs 15 days of antibiotics for VAP:

- A. The 8-day group had more antibiotic-free days. — T / F
- B. It was conducted in the United States. — T / F
- C. 28-day mortality was similar in both groups. — T / F
- D. Recurrence was lower with 8 days among patients with non-fermenting Gram-negative bacilli. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.** C – 18.8 vs 17.2 %. D – recurrence was higher with 8 days (40.6 vs 25.4 %). B – France.

</details>

**5.** Which patient would NOT have been eligible for the BALANCE trial?

- A. Pseudomonas aeruginosa bacteraemia from pneumonia
- B. E. coli bacteraemia from cholangitis after drainage
- C. Staphylococcus aureus bacteraemia
- D. Enterococcus faecalis bacteraemia without endocarditis
- E. Klebsiella pneumoniae bacteraemia from a urinary source in the ICU

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** *S. aureus* (and *S. lugdunensis*) and candidaemia were excluded.

</details>

**6.** According to SSC 2021:

- A. Double Gram-negative coverage should be continued after susceptibilities are known to prevent resistance. — T / F
- B. Longer over shorter duration of therapy is suggested when source control is adequate. — T / F
- C. Daily assessment for de-escalation is suggested. — T / F
- D. Procalcitonin plus clinical evaluation is suggested to decide when to discontinue antimicrobials. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – F, C – T, D – T.** A – de-escalate once susceptibilities are known. B – SSC 2021 suggests shorter over longer duration.

</details>

**7.** In the SAPS trial (Lancet Infect Dis 2016), procalcitonin-guided discontinuation in Dutch ICUs resulted in:

- A. No change in antibiotic duration
- B. Shorter antibiotic duration and lower 28-day mortality
- C. Shorter duration but higher 1-year mortality
- D. Increased relapse of infection requiring early termination
- E. Longer antibiotic duration and higher mortality

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Median 5 vs 7 days; 28-d mortality 19.6 vs 25.0 %.

</details>

**8.** Antimicrobial de-escalation:

- A. Is synonymous with shortening treatment duration. — T / F
- B. Refers only to switching from intravenous to oral administration. — T / F
- C. Includes adding an agent to the empirical regimen when the patient deteriorates. — T / F
- D. Has high-quality evidence demonstrating a reduction in antimicrobial resistance (ESICM/ESCMID 2020). — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – F, C – F, D – F.** De-escalation = narrowing the spectrum based on cultures and/or stopping one agent of a combination; it is not synonymous with shortening duration or IV-to-oral switch (B), and adding an agent (C) is escalation. D – high-quality research on the effect on AMR is lacking.

</details>

**9.** A ventilated patient treated for pneumonia has a PCT peak of 12 µg/L on day 1; on day 5 PCT is 2.0 µg/L and he is clinically improving. Based on the PRORATA/SAPS stopping rules:

- A. Stopping can be considered because PCT has fallen by more than 80 % from peak
- B. Antibiotics should be escalated
- C. CRP must be measured before any decision
- D. Antibiotics cannot be stopped because PCT is > 0.5 µg/L
- E. PCT is not interpretable in ventilated patients

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** 2/12 = 17 % of peak → a fall of 83 % (≥ 80 %).

</details>

**10.** Regarding biomarkers in antibiotic stewardship:

- A. In PRORATA, 60-day mortality was numerically higher in the PCT group. — T / F
- B. PCT is primarily used in the ICU to decide when to stop antibiotics. — T / F
- C. In ADAPT-Sepsis, a CRP-guided protocol did not shorten antibiotic duration. — T / F
- D. PCT guidance requires no adherence to the algorithm to be effective. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.** A – 30.0 vs 26.1 %, NS.

</details>

**O1. (open)** On day 3 of broad-spectrum therapy for E. coli bacteraemia from pyelonephritis in the ICU, outline your "antibiotic time-out", including de-escalation and the planned duration of therapy, with supporting evidence.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Reassess the diagnosis (cultures, imaging, alternative causes) and confirm source control (drainage, catheter removal; exclude abscess).
- De-escalate according to susceptibility: narrow the spectrum and stop unnecessary agents (e.g. anti-MRSA drug, second gram-negative drug); SSC 2021 suggests daily reassessment (weak).
- De-escalation is distinct from shortening duration – a separate decision.
- Plan a stop date: 7 days for uncomplicated G− bacteraemia (BALANCE: 90-d mortality 14.5 vs 16.1 %, non-inferior; more antibiotic-free days).
- BALANCE does not apply to S. aureus, S. lugdunensis or candidaemia, prosthetic material or infections needing long therapy.
- Serial PCT as an aid to stopping (PCT < 0.5 µg/L or ≥ 80 % fall from peak) combined with clinical evaluation.
- Rationale: each extra day of broad spectrum increases selection pressure, MDR colonisation, C. difficile and toxicity.

</details>

**O2. (open)** Compare PRORATA, SAPS and ADAPT-Sepsis and explain what they tell us about using biomarkers to guide antibiotic duration in the ICU.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- PRORATA (630, France): stop at PCT < 0.5 µg/L or ≥ 80 % fall; +2.7 antibiotic-free days (14.3 vs 11.6); 28-d mortality 21.2 vs 20.4 % (non-inferior); 60-d mortality numerically higher 30.0 vs 26.1 %.
- SAPS (1575, Netherlands): median duration 5 vs 7 days; 28-d mortality lower 19.6 vs 25.0 % – surprising, mechanism unexplained.
- ADAPT-Sepsis (2760, 41 UK ICUs): PCT shortened duration by 0.88 days (9.8 vs 10.7) with non-inferior mortality; CRP did not shorten therapy and non-inferiority was not shown.
- PCT safely shortens exposure by ~1–2 days; CRP is not a substitute for PCT.
- Effect depends on adherence to the algorithm, often overruled in trials.
- PCT is for stopping, not starting antibiotics (SSC 2021: PCT + clinical evaluation, weak, low quality).

</details>

**O1. (open)** Explain why piperacillin-tazobactam should not be used for ESBL bacteraemia even when the isolate is reported susceptible, and outline your targeted therapy for 3GC-resistant Enterobacterales according to ESCMID 2022.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:

**O2. (open)** Outline how the carbapenemase type determines your choice of agent for CRE, and explain the pharmacological rationale for combining aztreonam with avibactam.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- KPC (class A serine): meropenem-vaborbactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, imipenem-relebactam (IDSA 2024 preferred options).

- Cefiderocol: siderophore cephalosporin entering via iron transport, stable against all carbapenemase classes.
- Monotherapy if susceptible to ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem-vaborbactam or cefiderocol (strong, low); ≥ 2 active drugs only if only old drugs are active; no tigecycline for BSI or HAP/VAP.

</details>
