## W02 · D1 – Antibiotic PK/PD in the critically ill and prolonged β-lactam infusion

**1.** A 24-year-old man with polytrauma develops ventilator-associated pneumonia on day 6. Serum creatinine is 55 µmol/L. Measured 8-h urinary creatinine clearance is 190 mL/min/1.73 m². Which statement is most accurate?

- A. He has augmented renal clearance and is at risk of subtherapeutic β-lactam concentrations
- B. Aminoglycoside trough monitoring is unnecessary in this patient
- C. Standard meropenem dosing will reliably achieve target concentrations because renal function is normal
- D. The loading dose of meropenem should be reduced
- E. The Cockcroft-Gault equation is the best method to detect this condition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** A measured CrCl > 130 mL/min/1.73 m² from a timed (8–24 h) urine collection defines augmented renal clearance (ARC); 190 mL/min/1.73 m² in a young polytrauma patient is the classic picture (ICU incidence 14–80 %). ARC accelerates elimination of renally cleared hydrophilic β-lactams, so trough concentrations fall below the MIC – manage with higher/more frequent doses or continuous infusion plus TDM.

**Why not the others:**
- B – ARC also increases aminoglycoside clearance, and routine aminoglycoside TDM is recommended regardless (efficacy and toxicity).
- C – a "normal" creatinine hides ARC; standard meropenem dosing frequently underexposes these patients.
- D – the loading dose is determined by Vd, not by renal function, and is never reduced.
- E – Cockcroft-Gault and other estimating equations do not reliably detect ARC; a measured urine collection is required.

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Antimicrobial TDM Position Paper, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**2.** Regarding pharmacokinetic changes in septic shock:

- A. The volume of distribution of hydrophilic antibiotics is typically increased. — T / F
- B. The loading dose of a β-lactam should be reduced in acute kidney injury. — T / F
- C. Aminoglycosides and β-lactams are lipophilic antibiotics. — T / F
- D. Hypoalbuminaemia may increase the clearance of highly protein-bound drugs such as ceftriaxone. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – T.**

- A (True) – capillary leak, fluid resuscitation, oedema, drains and ECMO expand the extracellular space in which hydrophilic drugs distribute → low concentrations after the first dose.
- B (False) – the loading dose depends on Vd and is independent of renal function; only maintenance dosing is adjusted, and not reflexively in the first 24–48 h, as septic AKI is often transient.
- C (False) – β-lactams and aminoglycosides are hydrophilic (extracellular distribution, renal elimination); fluoroquinolones and macrolides are lipophilic.
- D (True) – hypoalbuminaemia raises the free fraction of highly bound drugs (ceftriaxone, ertapenem, flucloxacillin, teicoplanin) → larger Vd and faster clearance → low trough levels.

**EDIC trap:** early in sepsis the main β-lactam risk is underdosing, not toxicity.

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**3.** The pharmacodynamic index that best predicts the efficacy of meropenem is:

- A. Post-antibiotic effect duration
- B. Trough concentration relative to the toxic threshold
- C. Cmax/MIC
- D. AUC₀₋₂₄/MIC
- E. Percentage of the dosing interval during which the free concentration exceeds the MIC (% fT>MIC)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** β-lactams (including meropenem) show time-dependent killing; efficacy correlates with the percentage of the dosing interval during which the free concentration exceeds the MIC (% fT>MIC). Classic animal targets were 40–70 % fT>MIC; for the critically ill the 2020 Position Paper proposes 100 % fT>MIC – the rationale for prolonged/continuous infusion after a loading bolus.

**Why not the others:**
- A – the post-antibiotic effect modifies dosing but is not the PK/PD index; β-lactams have little PAE against Gram-negatives.
- B – trough relative to a toxic threshold is a safety parameter (aminoglycosides), not an efficacy index.
- C – Cmax/MIC (≥ 8–10) predicts aminoglycoside efficacy.
- D – AUC₀₋₂₄/MIC is the index for vancomycin (≥ 400) and fluoroquinolones.

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**4.** Regarding the BLING III trial:

- A. It enrolled fewer than 1000 critically ill adults with sepsis. — T / F
- B. Piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem was given by continuous or intermittent infusion. — T / F
- C. 90-day all-cause mortality was significantly lower with continuous infusion. — T / F
- D. Clinical cure was similar in both groups. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – F, D – F.**

- A (False) – BLING III randomised 7031 patients (primary analysis) in 104 ICUs, the largest β-lactam infusion trial.
- B (True) – piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem by continuous vs intermittent infusion, open-label.
- C (False) – 90-day mortality 24.9 vs 26.8 %, OR 0.91 (95 % CI 0.81–1.01), p = 0.08 – not significant, although the CI includes a clinically important benefit.
- D (False) – clinical cure was higher with continuous infusion (55.7 vs 50.0 %); new MDR colonisation/infection and *C. difficile* did not differ.

**EDIC trap:** BLING III was formally negative; the positive (Bayesian) result was the companion meta-analysis in the same JAMA issue.

**Ref:** Dulhunty JM et al., BLING III, JAMA 2024.

</details>

**5.** According to the 2020 ESICM/ESCMID-endorsed Position Paper on antimicrobial TDM, routine TDM in critically ill patients is recommended for:

- A. Echinocandins and fluconazole
- B. Only vancomycin and aminoglycosides
- C. Daptomycin and polymyxin B
- D. Aminoglycosides, β-lactams, vancomycin, teicoplanin, linezolid and voriconazole
- E. Fluoroquinolones and colistin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** The joint ESICM/ESCMID/IATDMCT/ISAC Position Paper recommends routine TDM in the critically ill for aminoglycosides, β-lactams, linezolid, teicoplanin, vancomycin and voriconazole – drugs with highly variable ICU pharmacokinetics, defined exposure targets and/or a narrow therapeutic window.

**Why not the others:**
- A – for echinocandins and fluconazole (most antifungals) the panel neither recommends nor advises against TDM; voriconazole is the only antifungal on the list.
- B – too narrow: it omits β-lactams, linezolid, teicoplanin and voriconazole.
- C – daptomycin and polymyxin B: no recommendation for or against.
- E – fluoroquinolones and colistin: no recommendation for or against.

**EDIC trap:** "neither recommends nor advises against" is not the same as "recommends against".

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**6.** Regarding the 2024 JAMA meta-analysis of prolonged vs intermittent β-lactam infusion in sepsis:

- A. It used a Bayesian primary analysis. — T / F
- B. Prolonged infusion was associated with lower 90-day mortality. — T / F
- C. The certainty of evidence for mortality was rated as high. — T / F
- D. It included 18 randomised trials with over 9000 patients. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – T.**

- A (True) – the primary analysis was Bayesian, reporting a risk ratio with a 95 % credible interval and a posterior probability of benefit (99.1 %).
- B (True) – 90-day mortality RR 0.86 (95 % CrI 0.72–0.98) favouring prolonged infusion.
- C (True) – certainty of the mortality benefit was rated high (GRADE); clinical cure also improved (RR 1.16) and ICU mortality fell.
- D (True) – 18 RCTs with 9108 patients, including BLING III; "prolonged" covered both continuous and extended infusion.

**EDIC trap:** K′ items with all four statements true do occur – do not force a "False".

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., JAMA 2024.

</details>

**7.** Which vancomycin target is recommended for efficacy in critically ill patients?

- A. Trough 5–10 mg/L
- B. Peak 40 mg/L
- C. AUC₀₋₂₄/MIC ≥ 400
- D. Time above MIC 100 %
- E. Cmax/MIC ≥ 10

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** Vancomycin efficacy is best predicted by AUC₀₋₂₄/MIC ≥ 400 (assuming MIC 1 mg/L). The trough is a poor surrogate for AUC, and targeting troughs of 15–20 mg/L increases nephrotoxicity without better efficacy; AUC-guided dosing (Bayesian or two-level sampling) is now standard.

**Why not the others:**
- A – a trough of 5–10 mg/L is too low for serious infection, and troughs are not the efficacy target anyway.
- B – vancomycin is not peak-driven; a peak of 40 mg/L is not a target.
- D – 100 % fT>MIC is the β-lactam target in the critically ill.
- E – Cmax/MIC ≥ 8–10 is the aminoglycoside efficacy target.

**EDIC trap:** "trough 15–20 mg/L" is the outdated answer.

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020; Rybak MJ et al., ASHP/IDSA/PIDS/SIDP vancomycin consensus, Am J Health Syst Pharm 2020.

</details>

**8.** Regarding aminoglycoside dosing in the ICU:

- A. Dosing in three divided daily doses is preferred to once-daily administration. — T / F
- B. Efficacy is concentration-dependent. — T / F
- C. A high trough concentration is the target for efficacy. — T / F
- D. A Cmax/MIC ratio of about 8–10 is a commonly used efficacy target. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – F, D – T.**

- A (False) – once-daily high-dose (extended-interval) dosing is preferred: it maximises Cmax/MIC and allows a low trough, reducing nephro- and ototoxicity.
- B (True) – aminoglycoside killing is concentration-dependent.
- C (False) – a high trough predicts toxicity, not efficacy; targets are amikacin < 2.5 mg/L and gentamicin/tobramycin < 0.5 mg/L.
- D (True) – Cmax/MIC ≥ 8–10 is the usual efficacy target (alternatively AUC 80–120 mg·h/L).

**EDIC trap:** peak = efficacy, trough = toxicity for aminoglycosides.

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**9.** In BLING II (AJRCCM 2015), continuous vs intermittent β-lactam infusion in 432 patients with severe sepsis resulted in:

- A. Higher rates of Clostridioides difficile infection with continuous infusion
- B. A significant reduction in 90-day mortality
- C. No significant difference in ICU-free days
- D. Early termination for harm
- E. Significantly more ICU-free days with continuous infusion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** BLING II (432 patients with severe sepsis, multicentre) found no difference in ICU-free days to day 28 (18 vs 20 days, p = 0.38), clinical cure or survival between continuous and intermittent β-lactam infusion. It was underpowered for mortality and led to the much larger BLING III.

**Why not the others:**
- A – no excess of *C. difficile* infection was reported (BLING III also found none).
- B – there was no significant reduction in 90-day mortality.
- D – the trial was completed, not stopped for harm.
- E – ICU-free days were numerically lower with continuous infusion (18 vs 20) and the difference was not significant.

**Ref:** Dulhunty JM et al., BLING II, Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2015.

</details>

**10.** When starting a prolonged meropenem infusion in septic shock:

- A. SSC 2026 recommends prolonged infusion for maintenance with a strong recommendation. — T / F
- B. Drug stability at room temperature should be considered. — T / F
- C. An initial loading bolus should precede the infusion. — T / F
- D. Prolonged infusion makes TDM unnecessary in patients on ECMO. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – SSC 2026 recommends prolonged β-lactam infusion for maintenance after an initial bolus – strong, moderate certainty (upgraded from weak in SSC 2021 after BLING III and the meta-analysis).
- B (True) – meropenem has limited stability at room temperature → more frequent syringe changes or an extended (e.g. 3-h) rather than 24-h infusion.
- C (True) – without a loading bolus, the target concentration takes hours to reach.
- D (False) – ECMO (circuit sequestration, ↑ Vd), RRT, ARC, obesity and high-MIC pathogens are exactly where β-lactam TDM is most useful, including with prolonged infusion.

**Ref:** Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026 (Prescott HC et al., Crit Care Med 2026); Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

## W02 · D2 – De-escalation, duration of therapy and procalcitonin

**1.** A 60-year-old with E. coli bacteraemia from pyelonephritis is afebrile and haemodynamically stable on day 5 of appropriate therapy, with no abscess on imaging. According to the BALANCE trial, the most appropriate total antibiotic duration is:

- A. 7 days
- B. 14 days
- C. Until procalcitonin is undetectable
- D. 21 days
- E. 3 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** BALANCE randomised 3608 hospitalised patients with bacteraemia (~55 % in ICU) to 7 vs 14 days: 90-day mortality 14.5 vs 16.1 % (difference −1.6 points, non-inferiority margin 4 points), relapse 2.6 vs 2.2 %, and more antibiotic-free days. A stable patient with uncomplicated *E. coli* bacteraemia and no abscess is a typical eligible patient.

**Why not the others:**
- B – 14 days was not better and only adds antibiotic exposure.
- C – "undetectable PCT" is not a validated rule; PCT stopping rules use < 0.5 µg/L or a ≥ 80 % fall, as an aid to clinical judgement.
- D – 21 days has no role in uncomplicated Gram-negative bacteraemia.
- E – 3 days was not tested and is too short for bacteraemia.

**EDIC trap:** BALANCE does not apply to *S. aureus* bacteraemia or candidaemia.

**Ref:** BALANCE Investigators, N Engl J Med 2025.

</details>

**2.** Regarding the PRORATA trial:

- A. 28-day mortality was significantly lower in the PCT group. — T / F
- B. The PCT group had more antibiotic-free days by day 28. — T / F
- C. Antibiotics were discontinued only when PCT fell below 0.1 µg/L. — T / F
- D. Relapse of infection was significantly more frequent in the PCT group. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – F, D – F.**

- A (False) – 28-day mortality 21.2 vs 20.4 % – non-inferior (margin 10 %), not lower.
- B (True) – antibiotic-free days to day 28 were 14.3 vs 11.6 (difference 2.7 days, p < 0.0001).
- C (False) – stopping was encouraged at PCT < 0.5 µg/L or a fall ≥ 80 % from peak (strongly at < 0.25 µg/L), not < 0.1 µg/L.
- D (False) – relapse 6.5 vs 5.1 %, not significant.

**EDIC trap:** 60-day mortality was numerically higher with PCT (30.0 vs 26.1 %, NS).

**Ref:** Bouadma L et al., PRORATA, Lancet 2010.

</details>

**3.** In ADAPT-Sepsis (JAMA 2025), which strategy reduced total antibiotic duration while being non-inferior for 28-day mortality?

- A. Daily CRP-guided protocol
- B. Fixed 5-day course
- C. Both CRP and procalcitonin protocols
- D. Neither protocol
- E. Daily procalcitonin-guided protocol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** ADAPT-Sepsis (2760 adults, 41 UK ICUs, three arms) found that a daily procalcitonin-guided protocol shortened antibiotic duration (9.8 vs 10.7 days; difference 0.88 days, p = 0.01) with non-inferior 28-day mortality (20.9 vs 19.4 %). The CRP protocol did not shorten therapy.

**Why not the others:**
- A – the CRP protocol shortened duration by only 0.09 days and non-inferiority for mortality was not demonstrated.
- B – a fixed 5-day course was not one of the trial arms.
- C – only the PCT protocol achieved both outcomes.
- D – the PCT protocol was effective.

**EDIC trap:** CRP is not a substitute for PCT in guiding antibiotic duration.

**Ref:** Dark P et al., ADAPT-Sepsis, JAMA 2025.

</details>

**4.** Regarding the PneumA trial of 8 vs 15 days of antibiotics for VAP:

- A. The 8-day group had more antibiotic-free days. — T / F
- B. It was conducted in the United States. — T / F
- C. 28-day mortality was similar in both groups. — T / F
- D. Recurrence was lower with 8 days among patients with non-fermenting Gram-negative bacilli. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – the 8-day group had more antibiotic-free days without excess mortality.
- B (False) – PneumA was conducted in 51 French ICUs (401 patients).
- C (True) – 28-day mortality 18.8 vs 17.2 % – non-inferior.
- D (False) – with non-fermenting Gram-negative bacilli (e.g. *Pseudomonas*) recurrence was higher with 8 days (40.6 vs 25.4 %).

**EDIC trap:** the penalty of short courses for non-fermenters is more recurrence, not higher mortality (see also iDIAPASON).

**Ref:** Chastre J et al., PneumA, JAMA 2003.

</details>

**5.** Which patient would NOT have been eligible for the BALANCE trial?

- A. Pseudomonas aeruginosa bacteraemia from pneumonia
- B. E. coli bacteraemia from cholangitis after drainage

**Why not the others:**

- D – *E. faecalis* bacteraemia without endocarditis was eligible.

**Ref:** BALANCE Investigators, N Engl J Med 2025.

**6.** According to SSC 2021:

- A. Double Gram-negative coverage should be continued after susceptibilities are known to prevent resistance. — T / F
- B. Longer over shorter duration of therapy is suggested when source control is adequate. — T / F
- C. Daily assessment for de-escalation is suggested. — T / F

**7.** In the SAPS trial (Lancet Infect Dis 2016), procalcitonin-guided discontinuation in Dutch ICUs resulted in:

- A. No change in antibiotic duration
- B. Shorter antibiotic duration and lower 28-day mortality
- C. Shorter duration but higher 1-year mortality
- D. Increased relapse of infection requiring early termination
- E. Longer antibiotic duration and higher mortality

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** SAPS randomised 1575 patients in Dutch ICUs with non-binding advice to stop antibiotics at a ≥ 80 % fall from peak or PCT ≤ 0.5 µg/L. Median duration fell from 7 to 5 days (DDD 7.5 vs 9.3) and 28-day mortality was lower (19.6 vs 25.0 %, p = 0.0122); 1-year mortality 34.8 vs 40.9 %.

**Why not the others:**
- A – antibiotic duration was clearly shorter.
- C – 1-year mortality was lower, not higher, with PCT guidance.
- D – the trial was not stopped early for relapse.
- E – the opposite of the result.

**EDIC trap:** the SAPS mortality benefit is unexplained and was not reproduced by PRORATA or ADAPT-Sepsis – the consistent finding is safe shortening of therapy.

**Ref:** de Jong E et al., SAPS, Lancet Infect Dis 2016.

</details>

**8.** Antimicrobial de-escalation:

- A. Is synonymous with shortening treatment duration. — T / F
- B. Refers only to switching from intravenous to oral administration. — T / F
- C. Includes adding an agent to the empirical regimen when the patient deteriorates. — T / F
- D. Has high-quality evidence demonstrating a reduction in antimicrobial resistance (ESICM/ESCMID 2020). — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – F, C – F, D – F.**

- A (False) – de-escalation changes the spectrum or number of drugs; duration of therapy is a separate decision.
- B (False) – IV-to-oral switch is a different stewardship intervention; de-escalation means narrowing the spectrum according to cultures and/or stopping a component of a combination.
- C (False) – adding an agent when the patient deteriorates is escalation.
- D (False) – the ESICM/ESCMID position statement (13 statements, Delphi) stresses that high-quality research on the effect of de-escalation on resistance is lacking and that its definition varies.

**EDIC trap:** a K′ item with no true statement is possible.

**Ref:** Tabah A et al., ESICM/ESCMID position statement, Intensive Care Med 2020.

</details>

**9.** A ventilated patient treated for pneumonia has a PCT peak of 12 µg/L on day 1; on day 5 PCT is 2.0 µg/L and he is clinically improving. Based on the PRORATA/SAPS stopping rules:

- A. Stopping can be considered because PCT has fallen by more than 80 % from peak
- B. Antibiotics should be escalated
- C. CRP must be measured before any decision
- D. Antibiotics cannot be stopped because PCT is > 0.5 µg/L
- E. PCT is not interpretable in ventilated patients

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** 2.0/12 µg/L = 17 % of peak, i.e. an 83 % fall – this meets the "≥ 80 % fall from peak" criterion. The PRORATA/SAPS rules are either/or (PCT < 0.5 µg/L **or** ≥ 80 % fall), so with clinical improvement stopping can be considered even though PCT is still > 0.5 µg/L.

**Why not the others:**
- B – he is improving; there is no indication to escalate.
- C – CRP is not needed and is not a substitute for PCT (ADAPT-Sepsis).
- D – this ignores the relative-fall criterion.
- E – both PRORATA and SAPS included ventilated ICU patients.

**EDIC trap:** do the arithmetic – a fall to 2.0 from 12 is > 80 %.

**Ref:** Bouadma L et al., Lancet 2010; de Jong E et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2016.

</details>

- PCT safely shortens exposure by ~1–2 days; CRP is not a substitute for PCT.
- Effect depends on adherence to the algorithm, often overruled in trials.
- PCT is for stopping, not starting antibiotics (SSC 2021: PCT + clinical evaluation, weak, low quality).

- D. In AIDA, colistin plus meropenem did not reduce clinical failure compared with colistin alone. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – T.**

- A (True) – ESCMID 2022 conditionally recommends against cefiderocol for CRAB (low certainty), because of excess mortality in CREDIBLE-CR (*Acinetobacter*: 50 vs 18 %).
- B (True) – in ATTACK, 28-day mortality was 19 vs 32 % with sulbactam-durlobactam vs colistin (both + imipenem), with less nephrotoxicity.
- C (True) – sulbactam has intrinsic activity against *Acinetobacter* via PBP binding; durlobactam protects it from *Acinetobacter* β-lactamases (OXA).
- D (True) – AIDA: clinical failure 79 vs 73 %, RR 0.93 (0.83–1.03) – no significant benefit from adding meropenem.

**Ref:** Paul M et al., Clin Microbiol Infect 2022; Kaye KS et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2023; Paul M et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2018.

**11.** According to the 2020 Position Paper on antimicrobial TDM, augmented renal clearance is defined as:

- A. Urine output > 3 mL/kg/h
- B. Serum creatinine < 50 µmol/L
- C. Measured creatinine clearance > 130 mL/min/1.73 m² from an 8–24-h urine collection
- D. Cockcroft-Gault clearance > 100 mL/min
- E. Estimated GFR (CKD-EPI) > 90 mL/min

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** The 2020 Position Paper defines ARC as a measured creatinine clearance > 130 mL/min/1.73 m² from an 8–24-h urine collection. It occurs in 14–80 % of ICU subgroups, especially young polytrauma, TBI, sepsis and burns patients, and predisposes to subtherapeutic β-lactam concentrations.

**Why not the others:**
- A – urine output does not reflect creatinine clearance; polyuria is not the definition.
- B – a low serum creatinine may suggest ARC (or low muscle mass) but does not define it.
- D – Cockcroft-Gault does not reliably detect ARC, and the threshold is wrong.
- E – estimating equations such as CKD-EPI assume steady state and miss ARC; > 90 mL/min is simply normal.

**Ref:** Abdul-Aziz MH et al., Intensive Care Med 2020.

**12.** A patient with suspected HAP has no septic shock, no MDR risk factors and is in a low-resistance unit.

- A. A lower respiratory tract sample should be obtained to guide therapy. — T / F
- B. A narrow-spectrum agent such as ertapenem or ceftriaxone is acceptable. — T / F
- C. Empirical MRSA coverage is mandatory. — T / F
- D. If HAP probability is low and the patient does not deteriorate within 72 h, continuing antibiotics beyond 3 days is suggested. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – F, D – F.**

- A (True) – a lower respiratory tract sample should be obtained to narrow empirical therapy (strong, low).
- B (True) – in low-risk patients a narrow-spectrum agent such as ertapenem, ceftriaxone, cefotaxime, moxifloxacin or levofloxacin is suggested (weak, very low).
- C (False) – MRSA coverage is indicated only with MRSA risk factors or high local MRSA prevalence, not routinely.
- D (False) – with a low probability of HAP and no deterioration within 72 h, ERS/ESICM 2017 suggests against continuing antibiotics beyond 3 days (weak, low).

**Ref:** Torres A et al., ERS/ESICM/ESCMID/ALAT guidelines, Eur Respir J 2017.

**13.** A 70-year-old with bacteraemia due to Enterobacter cloacae (ceftriaxone-susceptible in vitro, cefepime MIC 0.5 mg/L, no ESBL) from a biliary source after drainage. The most appropriate definitive therapy is:

- A. Cefepime
- B. Ampicillin
- C. Ceftriaxone
- D. Colistin
- E. Ceftazidime-avibactam

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** *Enterobacter cloacae* has an inducible chromosomal AmpC, so ceftriaxone may select derepressed mutants and fail despite in vitro susceptibility. Cefepime is stable to AmpC and, with a low MIC (0.5 mg/L) and no ESBL, is the preferred definitive agent after source control (IDSA 2024).

**Why not the others:**
- B – *Enterobacter* is intrinsically resistant to ampicillin (AmpC).
- C – IDSA 2024 advises against ceftriaxone for invasive infections with AmpC-prone organisms.
- D – colistin is a toxic last-resort agent, inappropriate when β-lactams are active.
- E – ceftazidime-avibactam should be reserved for carbapenem-resistant organisms.

**Ref:** Tamma PD et al., IDSA 2024 Guidance, Clin Infect Dis 2024.

**14.** Regarding prolonged β-lactam infusion:

- A. SSC 2026 recommends (strong) prolonged infusion for maintenance after an initial bolus. — T / F
- B. Prolonged infusion maximises Cmax/MIC. — T / F
- C. The 2024 JAMA meta-analysis rated the certainty of a mortality benefit as high. — T / F
- D. BLING II showed significantly more ICU-free days with continuous infusion. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**15.** In the AMIKINHAL trial, the intervention that reduced VAP incidence was:

- A. Chlorhexidine oral care
- B. Selective digestive decontamination
- C. Intravenous amikacin prophylaxis at intubation
- D. Inhaled amikacin once daily for 3 days started after 72–96 h of ventilation
- E. Subglottic secretion drainage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** AMIKINHAL randomised 847 patients ventilated for 72–96 h to inhaled amikacin 20 mg/kg ideal body weight once daily for 3 days or placebo; VAP by day 28 fell from 22 to 15 % and infection-related ventilator-associated complications from 26 to 18 %. It was not powered for mortality.

**Why not the others:**
- A – chlorhexidine oral care was not tested; ERS/ESICM 2017 makes no recommendation on it.
- B – SDD was not tested; ERS/ESICM suggests SOD (not SDD) only in low-resistance settings.
- C – the intervention was inhaled, not intravenous, amikacin, started after 72–96 h of ventilation rather than at intubation.
- E – subglottic secretion drainage is a bundle element but not the AMIKINHAL intervention.

**Ref:** Ehrmann S et al., AMIKINHAL, N Engl J Med 2023.

</details>
