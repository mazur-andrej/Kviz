## W10 · D1 – ARDS: Berlin vs new global definition, pathophysiology, phenotypes

**1.** A 58-year-old with pneumococcal pneumonia is on high-flow nasal oxygen 50 L/min, FiO₂ 0.7, PaO₂ 63 mmHg (SpO₂ 92 %). Chest radiograph shows bilateral opacities; echocardiography is normal. Which statement is correct?

- A. He meets the new global definition of ARDS (non-intubated category)
- B. He meets the Berlin definition of moderate ARDS
- C. ARDS cannot be diagnosed without a CT scan
- D. He cannot have ARDS until he is intubated
- E. He meets the global definition of severe ARDS

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** P/F = 63/0.7 = 90 mmHg on HFNO ≥ 30 L/min, with bilateral opacities, a clear risk factor (pneumonia) and a normal echo – this fulfils the non-intubated category of the 2024 global definition.

**Why not the others:**
- B – Berlin requires PEEP/CPAP ≥ 5 cmH₂O; HFNO has no defined PEEP, so Berlin criteria are not met.
- C – chest X-ray (or lung ultrasound in the global definition) is sufficient; CT is never mandatory.
- D – both Berlin (NIV/CPAP) and the global definition (HFNO, NIV) allow ARDS without intubation.
- E – non-intubated patients are not stratified into mild/moderate/severe, because FiO₂ on HFNO is imprecise.

**EDIC trap:** a P/F < 100 tempts "severe", but severity grading applies only to intubated patients.

**Ref:** Matthay MA et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2024;209:37–47.

</details>

**2.** Regarding the Berlin definition of ARDS:

- A. The term "acute lung injury" was abandoned. — T / F
- B. Four ancillary variables for severe ARDS improved predictive validity and were retained. — T / F
- C. A minimum PEEP or CPAP of 10 cmH₂O is required when assessing PaO₂/FiO₂. — T / F
- D. A pulmonary artery wedge pressure ≤ 18 mmHg is required. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – F.**

- A (True) – "acute lung injury" (P/F 200–300 in AECC) was replaced by "mild ARDS".
- B (False) – the ancillary variables (compliance ≤ 40 mL/cmH₂O, PEEP ≥ 10, corrected minute ventilation ≥ 10 L/min, radiographic extent) added no predictive validity and were dropped.
- C (False) – the minimum is PEEP/CPAP ≥ 5 cmH₂O (CPAP allowed only in mild ARDS).
- D (False) – the PCWP criterion was removed, because ARDS and raised left atrial pressure can coexist; oedema must only be "not fully explained" by cardiac failure or fluid overload.

**EDIC trap:** PCWP ≤ 18 mmHg belongs to the AECC 1994 definition, not Berlin.

**Ref:** ARDS Definition Task Force, JAMA 2012;307:2526–2533.

</details>

**3.** In the new global definition of ARDS, the SpO₂/FiO₂ ratio may replace PaO₂/FiO₂ only if:

- A. SpO₂ is ≤ 97 %
- B. SpO₂ is ≥ 98 %
- C. The patient is intubated
- D. Haemoglobin is above 100 g/L
- E. The patient is in a high-income country

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** Above SpO₂ 97 % the oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve is flat, so a large range of PaO₂ values corresponds to almost the same SpO₂ and S/F no longer reflects oxygenation. The global definition therefore accepts S/F ≤ 315 only when SpO₂ is ≤ 97 %; FiO₂ should be titrated down until SpO₂ ≤ 97 % before calculating it.

**Why not the others:**
- B – SpO₂ ≥ 98 % is exactly the range in which S/F is invalid.
- C – S/F is allowed in all categories, including non-intubated and resource-limited settings.
- D – haemoglobin concentration is not a criterion (dyshaemoglobinaemias such as COHb/MetHb are the caveat).
- E – S/F is allowed in any setting; in resource-limited settings it is simply the main tool.

**Ref:** Matthay MA et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2024;209:37–47.

</details>

**4.** Regarding the modification of the global ARDS definition for resource-limited settings:

- A. Lung ultrasound may not be used for imaging. — T / F
- B. SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 400 is used. — T / F
- C. A minimum PEEP is not required. — T / F
- D. A minimum oxygen flow rate is not required. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – F, C – T, D – T.**

- A (False) – bilateral B-lines and/or consolidations on lung ultrasound by a trained operator are acceptable imaging in every category, and are especially useful where X-ray or CT is unavailable.
- B (False) – the oxygenation criterion is SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315, valid only with SpO₂ ≤ 97 %.
- C (True) – neither PEEP nor any specific respiratory support is required in resource-limited settings.
- D (True) – no minimum oxygen flow rate is required, unlike the non-intubated category (HFNO ≥ 30 L/min).

**EDIC trap:** the "no PEEP, no flow" relaxation applies only to the resource-limited category; elsewhere HFNO < 30 L/min does not qualify.

**Ref:** Matthay MA et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2024;209:37–47.

</details>

**5.** In the data used to validate the Berlin definition, hospital mortality for severe ARDS was approximately:

- A. 15 %
- B. 45 %
- C. 32 %
- D. 70 %
- E. 27 %

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** In the 4188-patient validation cohort of the Berlin definition, mortality rose with severity: 27 % (mild), 32 % (moderate) and 45 % (severe). Median duration of ventilation in survivors also increased (5, 7 and 9 days). Predictive validity was only marginally better than AECC (AUROC 0.577 vs 0.536).

**Why not the others:**
- A – 15 % is far below even the mild category.
- C – 32 % is the mortality of moderate ARDS.
- D – 70 % overstates mortality in contemporary severe ARDS cohorts.
- E – 27 % is the mortality of mild ARDS.

**EDIC trap:** do not confuse with LUNG SAFE hospital mortality (34.9 / 40.3 / 46.1 %).

**Ref:** ARDS Definition Task Force, JAMA 2012;307:2526–2533.

</details>

**6.** Findings of the LUNG SAFE study include:

- A. ARDS accounted for about 10 % of ICU admissions. — T / F
- B. Plateau pressure was measured in about 90 % of patients. — T / F
- C. Prone positioning was used in a minority (about 16 %) of severe ARDS. — T / F
- D. ARDS was recognised by clinicians in nearly all severe cases (> 95 %). — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – ARDS represented 10.4 % of ICU admissions and 23.4 % of mechanically ventilated patients across 459 ICUs in 50 countries.
- B (False) – plateau pressure was measured in only about 40 % of patients with ARDS.
- C (True) – prone positioning was used in only 16.3 % of patients with severe ARDS.
- D (False) – clinician recognition ranged from 51 % in mild to 79 % in severe ARDS; overall ARDS was under-recognised.

**EDIC trap:** LUNG SAFE is the source for "under-recognised and under-treated" – fewer than two thirds received VT ≤ 8 mL/kg PBW.

**Ref:** Bellani G et al. (LUNG SAFE), JAMA 2016;315:788–800.

</details>

**7.** The main mechanism of hypoxaemia in early ARDS that responds poorly to increasing FiO₂ is:

- A. Low inspired oxygen pressure
- B. Hypoventilation
- C. Diffusion limitation
- D. Intrapulmonary shunt
- E. Increased alveolar dead space

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** In early ARDS, flooded and collapsed alveoli remain perfused but are not ventilated, creating a true intrapulmonary shunt. Blood passing through shunt units never meets alveolar gas, so raising FiO₂ barely improves PaO₂ once the shunt fraction exceeds ~30 %. Recruitment (PEEP, prone positioning) rather than FiO₂ treats this mechanism.

**Why not the others:**
- A – low inspired PO₂ (altitude) is not the mechanism in ARDS and is fully FiO₂-responsive.
- B – hypoventilation causes hypercapnic hypoxaemia correctable by modest FiO₂.
- C – diffusion limitation plays a minor role and also responds to extra oxygen.
- E – increased dead space mainly causes hypercapnia; it is prognostic but not the main cause of refractory hypoxaemia.

**Ref:** Gattinoni L, Pesenti A, Intensive Care Med 2005;31:776–784.

</details>

**8.** Regarding the "baby lung" concept:

- A. The low respiratory system compliance mainly reflects a small amount of aerated lung rather than intrinsically stiff lung. — T / F
- B. Even a tidal volume of 6 mL/kg PBW may cause excessive strain when the aerated lung is very small. — T / F
- C. In the supine position, collapse predominates in dependent (dorsal) regions. — T / F
- D. Ventilator-induced lung injury includes volutrauma, atelectrauma and biotrauma. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – T.**

- A (True) – CT studies show that compliance correlates with the size of the aerated lung; the "baby lung" itself has near-normal specific compliance.
- B (True) – strain = VT / end-expiratory aerated lung volume, so even 6 mL/kg PBW may overdistend a very small baby lung (the rationale for limiting driving pressure).
- C (True) – the weight of the oedematous lung (superimposed pressure) produces dorsal, dependent atelectasis supine, with ventral hyperinflation.
- D (True) – VILI comprises volutrauma, barotrauma, atelectrauma and biotrauma (mediator release contributing to MODS).

**EDIC trap:** all-true K′ items exist; judge each statement separately.

**Ref:** Gattinoni L, Pesenti A, Intensive Care Med 2005;31:776–784; Slutsky AS, Ranieri VM, N Engl J Med 2013;369:2126–2136.

</details>

**9.** Compared with the hypoinflammatory subphenotype, the hyperinflammatory ARDS subphenotype is characterised by:

- A. Less frequent vasopressor use
- B. Higher mortality
- C. Exclusively pulmonary (direct) aetiology
- D. Higher serum bicarbonate
- E. Lower plasma IL-6 and IL-8

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** In the latent class analysis of ARMA and ALVEOLI, the hyperinflammatory subphenotype (about one third of patients) had markedly higher mortality and fewer ventilator- and organ-failure-free days. It was also associated with higher plasma IL-6, IL-8 and sTNFR-1, more vasopressor use, lower bicarbonate and more sepsis.

**Why not the others:**
- A – vasopressor use was more frequent, not less.
- C – the hyperinflammatory subphenotype is enriched for indirect (extrapulmonary) causes such as sepsis, not exclusively direct injury.
- D – serum bicarbonate is lower (more metabolic acidosis).
- E – IL-6 and IL-8 are higher; they are among the defining biomarkers.

**Ref:** Calfee CS et al., Lancet Respir Med 2014;2:611–620.

</details>

**10.** Regarding ARDS phenotyping and the ESICM 2023 guidelines:

- A. In ALVEOLI, the effect of higher versus lower PEEP appeared to differ between subphenotypes. — T / F
- B. Subphenotypes were first identified by latent class analysis of the LUNG SAFE cohort. — T / F
- C. ESICM 2023 gave no graded recommendation on the definition of ARDS. — T / F
- D. ESICM 2023 strongly recommends routine phenotype-guided PEEP selection. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – in ALVEOLI a significant interaction was found (p = 0.049 for mortality): hyperinflammatory patients appeared to benefit from higher PEEP; this is a post-hoc finding.
- B (False) – subphenotypes were first identified by latent class analysis of ARMA and ALVEOLI data (Calfee 2014), not LUNG SAFE.
- C (True) – ESICM 2023 discussed the definition (HFNO, S/F, ultrasound) without issuing a graded recommendation.
- D (False) – ESICM 2023 considers phenotyping hypothesis-generating and makes no recommendation to use it for routine PEEP or other treatment decisions.

**EDIC trap:** phenotype ≠ severity by P/F; it is based on biomarkers and clinical variables.

**Ref:** Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759; Calfee CS et al., Lancet Respir Med 2014;2:611–620.

</details>

**O2. (open)** Explain the "baby lung" concept and its implications for setting tidal volume in ARDS.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- In severe ARDS the normally aerated part of the lung is the size of a small child's lung.
- Low compliance reflects a small amount of aerated lung rather than intrinsically stiff lung.
- VT scaled to predicted body weight may still be excessive for a small aerated lung → excessive strain (volutrauma).
- Hence the logic of driving pressure (VT normalised to compliance).
- Gravitational distribution: dorsal atelectasis supine, ventral hyperinflation → rationale for prone positioning.
- VILI mechanisms: volutrauma, barotrauma, atelectrauma, biotrauma (→ MODS).

</details>


## W10 · D2 – Protective ventilation: ARMA, plateau and driving pressure, PBW

**1.** A 165-cm tall woman weighing 95 kg is intubated for ARDS. Her predicted body weight and the corresponding tidal volume at 6 mL/kg PBW are approximately:

- A. 50 kg and 300 mL
- B. 57 kg and 340 mL
- C. 75 kg and 450 mL
- D. 95 kg and 570 mL
- E. 62 kg and 370 mL

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** For women PBW = 45.5 + 0.91 × (height cm − 152.4) = 45.5 + 0.91 × 12.6 = 57.0 kg. At 6 mL/kg PBW the tidal volume is 57 × 6 ≈ 342 mL. Lung size depends on height and sex, not on actual weight, so the 95 kg is irrelevant.

**Why not the others:**
- A – 50 kg would correspond to a shorter woman (~157 cm); 300 mL underventilates slightly.
- C – 75 kg is the PBW of a 180-cm man, not a 165-cm woman.
- D – 95 kg is the actual weight; 570 mL equals ~10 mL/kg PBW, i.e. injurious.
- E – 62 kg results from using the male formula (50 + 11.5).

**EDIC trap:** short, obese women are the patients most often over-ventilated when VT is set by actual weight.

**Ref:** ARDS Network (ARMA), N Engl J Med 2000;342:1301–1308.

</details>

**2.** Regarding the ARMA trial (ARDS Network, 2000):

- A. Mortality was 31.0 % vs 39.8 % in favour of low tidal volume. — T / F
- B. The trial was stopped early for futility. — T / F
- C. The low tidal volume group was ventilated with 6 mL/kg actual body weight and plateau pressure ≤ 30 cmH₂O. — T / F
- D. The control group received 12 mL/kg predicted body weight with plateau pressure up to 50 cmH₂O. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – T.**

- A (True) – mortality before discharge home with unassisted breathing was 31.0 % vs 39.8 % (ARR ~9 %, NNT ~11).
- B (False) – the trial was stopped early for benefit after the fourth interim analysis (861 patients).
- C (False) – low VT was 6 mL/kg predicted (not actual) body weight, with Pplat ≤ 30 cmH₂O and VT down to 4 mL/kg if needed.
- D (True) – the control arm received 12 mL/kg PBW with Pplat ≤ 50 cmH₂O.

**EDIC trap:** a criticism of ARMA is that 12 mL/kg PBW exceeded usual practice of the time (~10 mL/kg actual weight).

**Ref:** ARDS Network (ARMA), N Engl J Med 2000;342:1301–1308.

</details>

**3.** A ventilated ARDS patient has VT 450 mL, PEEP 10 cmH₂O, peak pressure 38 cmH₂O and plateau pressure 28 cmH₂O. Which statement is correct?

- A. Driving pressure is 28 cmH₂O
- B. Driving pressure is 18 cmH₂O and static compliance is 25 mL/cmH₂O
- C. The 10 cmH₂O difference between peak and plateau reflects lung elastance
- D. Driving pressure is 10 cmH₂O
- E. Static compliance is 45 mL/cmH₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** Driving pressure = Pplat − PEEP = 28 − 10 = 18 cmH₂O. Static respiratory system compliance = VT/ΔP = 450/18 = 25 mL/cmH₂O. A ΔP of 18 exceeds the commonly quoted ~15 cmH₂O threshold even though Pplat is below 30.

**Why not the others:**
- A – 28 cmH₂O is the plateau pressure itself, not Pplat minus PEEP.
- C – Ppeak − Pplat (10 cmH₂O) is the resistive component (tube, secretions, bronchospasm), not elastance.
- D – 10 cmH₂O is the peak–plateau difference or the PEEP, not the driving pressure.
- E – 45 mL/cmH₂O would require ΔP of 10 cmH₂O.

**Ref:** Amato MBP et al., N Engl J Med 2015;372:747–755.

</details>

**4.** Regarding driving pressure (Amato, NEJM 2015):

- A. An increase of one standard deviation (~7 cmH₂O) was associated with a relative risk of death of about 1.4. — T / F
- B. Changes in PEEP were associated with survival only when they decreased driving pressure. — T / F
- C. It was derived from individual data of 3562 patients from nine randomised trials. — T / F
- D. The association disappeared in patients already receiving protective plateau pressures. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – each 1-SD increase in ΔP (~7 cmH₂O) was associated with RR of death 1.41 (95 % CI 1.31–1.51).
- B (True) – changes in VT or PEEP were associated with survival only when they lowered ΔP (mediation analysis).
- C (True) – individual data of 3562 patients from nine RCTs; it is an observational, not a randomised, analysis.
- D (False) – the association persisted in patients already receiving "protective" Pplat ≤ 30 and VT ≤ 7 mL/kg (RR 1.36).

**EDIC trap:** no RCT has yet tested a ΔP-targeted strategy, and ESICM 2023 makes no recommendation on ΔP.

**Ref:** Amato MBP et al., N Engl J Med 2015;372:747–755.

</details>

**5.** According to the ESICM 2023 ARDS guidelines, the tidal volume recommendation for ARDS not due to COVID-19 is:

- A. No recommendation because of insufficient evidence
- B. Tidal volume titrated to driving pressure < 10 cmH₂O, strong recommendation
- C. 4–8 mL/kg predicted body weight, strong recommendation
- D. 6 mL/kg actual body weight, conditional recommendation
- E. 8–10 mL/kg predicted body weight, strong recommendation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** ESICM 2023 recommends low tidal volume ventilation (4–8 mL/kg PBW) rather than larger VT in ARDS not due to COVID-19 – strong recommendation, high level of evidence. The same strong recommendation applies to COVID-19 ARDS with moderate certainty because of indirectness.

**Why not the others:**
- A – a strong recommendation exists, based on ARMA and subsequent meta-analyses.
- B – driving pressure is listed as a research question; no ΔP target is recommended.
- D – VT is always scaled to predicted, not actual, body weight.
- E – 8–10 mL/kg PBW is above the recommended range.

**Ref:** Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

</details>

**6.** Regarding plateau pressure measurement:

- A. It requires an end-inspiratory pause in a patient without active breathing effort. — T / F
- B. Active inspiratory effort may cause plateau pressure to underestimate lung stress. — T / F
- C. In morbid obesity, a plateau pressure slightly above 30 cmH₂O may correspond to an acceptable transpulmonary pressure. — T / F
- D. It reflects mainly airway resistance. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – Pplat requires an end-inspiratory hold at zero flow in a relaxed, passive patient, so that airway pressure equals alveolar pressure.
- B (True) – active inspiratory effort lowers pleural pressure, so measured Pplat underestimates the true transpulmonary pressure (lung stress).
- C (True) – with a stiff chest wall (obesity, intra-abdominal hypertension) part of the pressure distends the chest wall; oesophageal pressure can clarify transpulmonary pressure.
- D (False) – Pplat reflects elastic (lung + chest wall) recoil; airway resistance is reflected by Ppeak − Pplat.

**EDIC trap:** a "normal" Pplat with intrinsic PEEP still needs an end-expiratory hold to calculate true ΔP.

**Ref:** Slutsky AS, Ranieri VM, N Engl J Med 2013;369:2126–2136; Amato MBP et al., N Engl J Med 2015;372:747–755.

</details>

**7.** A 180-cm man with ARDS is ventilated with VT 600 mL. His driving pressure is 19 cmH₂O, plateau pressure 31 cmH₂O. The most appropriate first change is:

- A. Increase VT to improve CO₂ clearance
- B. Start inhaled nitric oxide
- C. Increase PEEP by 5 cmH₂O
- D. Reduce VT towards ~450 mL (6 mL/kg PBW) and increase respiratory rate
- E. Switch to pressure support ventilation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** PBW = 50 + 0.91 × (180 − 152.4) = 75.1 kg, so 600 mL ≈ 8 mL/kg PBW. With Pplat > 30 and ΔP 19 cmH₂O, the first step is to reduce VT towards 6 mL/kg (~450 mL) and increase the rate (up to 35/min) to maintain minute ventilation, accepting permissive hypercapnia.

**Why not the others:**
- A – increasing VT worsens volutrauma and raises Pplat further.
- B – inhaled NO improves oxygenation transiently but does not address injurious ventilation or mortality.
- C – raising PEEP at the same VT would push Pplat even higher unless substantial recruitment occurs.
- E – pressure support in early moderate–severe ARDS risks uncontrolled VT and P-SILI.

**Ref:** ARDS Network (ARMA), N Engl J Med 2000;342:1301–1308; Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

</details>

**8.** After increasing PEEP from 10 to 14 cmH₂O at constant VT 420 mL, plateau pressure rises from 26 to 28 cmH₂O. Which statements are correct?

- A. Static compliance decreased. — T / F
- B. Driving pressure increased from 16 to 18 cmH₂O. — T / F
- C. This pattern is consistent with alveolar recruitment. — T / F
- D. This pattern suggests that overdistension predominates. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

- A (False) – compliance rose from 420/16 = 26 to 420/14 = 30 mL/cmH₂O.
- B (False) – driving pressure fell from 16 (26 − 10) to 14 (28 − 14) cmH₂O.
- C (True) – a fall in ΔP (rise in compliance) after a PEEP increase at constant VT indicates that recruitment predominates.
- D (False) – overdistension would show a rise in ΔP and fall in compliance.

**EDIC trap:** a rising Pplat after a PEEP increase does not mean overdistension; look at ΔP, not Pplat alone.

**Ref:** Amato MBP et al., N Engl J Med 2015;372:747–755; Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

</details>

**9.** Permissive hypercapnia during lung-protective ventilation is most relevantly limited in a patient with:

- A. Type 2 diabetes
- B. Septic shock on low-dose noradrenaline
- C. Community-acquired pneumonia
- D. Raised intracranial pressure after traumatic brain injury
- E. Mild acute kidney injury

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasodilatation, increasing cerebral blood volume and intracranial pressure; after TBI this can reduce cerebral perfusion pressure. Raised ICP is therefore the main relative contraindication to permissive hypercapnia; severe pulmonary hypertension or RV failure (hypercapnia raises PVR) is the other.

**Why not the others:**
- A – type 2 diabetes does not interact relevantly with hypercapnia.
- B – mild acidosis is generally tolerated in septic shock on low-dose vasopressors.
- C – pneumonia is the commonest cause of ARDS and the typical setting for permissive hypercapnia.
- E – mild AKI impairs bicarbonate retention slightly but is not a contraindication.

**Ref:** ARDS Network (ARMA), N Engl J Med 2000;342:1301–1308.

</details>

**10.** Mechanisms of ventilator-induced lung injury include:

- A. Biotrauma with release of inflammatory mediators contributing to multiorgan failure — T / F
- B. Atelectrauma from cyclic opening and closing of unstable alveoli — T / F
- C. Volutrauma from excessive strain of the aerated lung — T / F
- D. Hypocapnia-induced alveolar collapse as the main mechanism — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – biotrauma: mechanical stress releases cytokines (e.g. IL-6) into the circulation, contributing to MODS; ARMA showed lower IL-6 with low VT.
- B (True) – atelectrauma: shear stress from cyclic opening and closing of unstable alveoli, mainly at low PEEP.
- C (True) – volutrauma: overdistension from excessive strain (VT relative to aerated lung volume).
- D (False) – hypocapnia is not a recognised main VILI mechanism; the fourth classical mechanism is barotrauma (pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum).

**Ref:** Slutsky AS, Ranieri VM, N Engl J Med 2013;369:2126–2136.

</details>

**11.** Which statement about driving pressure is most accurate?

- A. A target of < 15 cmH₂O is a strong ESICM 2023 recommendation
- B. It equals tidal volume divided by respiratory system compliance and was the ventilation variable most strongly associated with survival in a pooled analysis of nine RCTs
- C. It is independent of tidal volume
- D. It equals peak pressure minus PEEP
- E. Its association with mortality disappears when plateau pressure is ≤ 30 cmH₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** Driving pressure = Pplat − PEEP = VT/Crs, i.e. tidal volume normalised to the functional ("baby") lung size. In Amato's analysis of 3562 patients from nine RCTs it was the ventilatory variable most strongly associated with survival (RR 1.41 per 7 cmH₂O increase).

**Why not the others:**
- A – ESICM 2023 makes no recommendation on ΔP; it is a research priority.
- C – ΔP depends directly on VT (ΔP = VT/Crs).
- D – peak pressure includes the resistive component; ΔP uses plateau pressure.
- E – the association persisted in patients with Pplat ≤ 30 and VT ≤ 7 mL/kg (RR 1.36).

**EDIC trap:** the ~15 cmH₂O threshold is observational, not RCT-validated.

**Ref:** Amato MBP et al., N Engl J Med 2015;372:747–755; Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

**12.** A 35-year-old with influenza pneumonia–related ARDS has PaO₂/FiO₂ 65 mmHg for 4 hours despite VT 6 mL/kg PBW, PEEP 14 and prone positioning.

- A. ESICM 2023 recommends VV-ECMO for such patients in an ECMO centre meeting organisational standards. — T / F
- B. He already meets the EOLIA criterion PaO₂/FiO₂ < 50 mmHg for > 3 h. — T / F
- C. If PaO₂/FiO₂ stays < 80 mmHg for > 6 h, he will meet an EOLIA criterion. — T / F
- D. ESICM 2023 suggests ECCO₂R as an alternative to ECMO. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

- A (True) – ESICM recommends VV-ECMO for severe ARDS meeting EOLIA criteria, in an ECMO centre meeting organisational standards (strong, moderate certainty); early referral is reasonable.
- B (False) – P/F 65 is above 50, so the "P/F < 50 for > 3 h" criterion is not met.
- C (True) – P/F < 80 for > 6 h is an EOLIA criterion; he will meet it after 2 more hours.
- D (False) – ESICM recommends against ECCO₂R outside RCTs (strong, high certainty of no effect); it does not treat severe hypoxaemia.

**Ref:** Combes A et al. (EOLIA), N Engl J Med 2018;378:1965–1975; Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

</details>

**13.** In the LUNG SAFE study, which finding illustrates that ARDS is "under-recognised"?

- A. Clinician recognition ranged from about 51 % in mild to 79 % in severe ARDS
- B. Hospital mortality of severe ARDS was 46 %
- C. ARDS accounted for 10.4 % of ICU admissions
- D. Plateau pressure was measured in about 40 % of patients
- E. Most patients had PEEP < 12 cmH₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

**Why A:** In LUNG SAFE, clinicians recognised ARDS in only 51 % of mild and 79 % of severe cases (60 % overall); this is the direct measure of under-recognition. Under-recognition was linked to less use of protective measures.

**Why not the others:**
- B – 46 % mortality describes outcome, not recognition.
- C – 10.4 % of ICU admissions describes incidence.
- D – Pplat measurement in ~40 % illustrates under-treatment/under-monitoring.
- E – low PEEP levels also illustrate under-treatment, not recognition.

**EDIC trap:** "under-recognised" (diagnosis) and "under-treated" (VT, Pplat, proning) are separate LUNG SAFE messages.

**Ref:** Bellani G et al. (LUNG SAFE), JAMA 2016;315:788–800.

**14.** Regarding recruitment manoeuvres and PEEP in current guidelines:

- A. ATS 2024 suggests higher PEEP without recruitment manoeuvres in moderate–severe ARDS. — T / F
- B. The Briel meta-analysis found that higher PEEP reduced mortality most clearly in patients with PaO₂/FiO₂ > 200. — T / F
- C. ESICM 2023 suggests against routine brief high-pressure recruitment manoeuvres. — T / F
- D. ESICM 2023 strongly recommends against prolonged high-pressure recruitment manoeuvres (≥ 35 cmH₂O for ≥ 1 min). — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – T.**

- A (True) – ATS 2024 suggests higher PEEP without lung recruitment manoeuvres in moderate–severe ARDS (conditional, low–moderate certainty).
- B (False) – benefit was seen with P/F ≤ 200 (34.1 vs 39.1 %); with P/F > 200 there was a trend towards harm.
- C (True) – ESICM suggests against routine brief high-pressure RMs (conditional, high certainty of no effect).
- D (True) – ESICM strongly recommends against prolonged high-pressure RMs (≥ 35 cmH₂O for ≥ 1 min; moderate certainty).

**Ref:** Qadir N et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2024;209:24–36; Briel M et al., JAMA 2010;303:865–873; Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

**15.** A patient with severe ARDS and a raised intracranial pressure after traumatic brain injury requires ventilation. Which strategy element must be applied most cautiously?

- A. Monitoring driving pressure
- B. Measuring plateau pressure
- C. Head-of-bed elevation
- D. Using predicted body weight to set VT
- E. Permissive hypercapnia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasodilatation, increasing cerebral blood volume and ICP and threatening cerebral perfusion after TBI. Permissive hypercapnia must therefore be applied cautiously, with ICP monitoring and a PaCO₂ target agreed between lung and brain priorities; raised ICP is also a relative contraindication to prone positioning.

**Why not the others:**
- A – monitoring driving pressure is harmless and still useful.
- B – measuring plateau pressure carries no ICP risk.
- C – head-of-bed elevation (~30°) helps lower ICP and reduces VAP risk.
- D – VT by PBW is always appropriate and unrelated to ICP.

**Ref:** ARDS Network (ARMA), N Engl J Med 2000;342:1301–1308; Grasselli G et al., Intensive Care Med 2023;49:727–759.

</details>
