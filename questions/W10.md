## W10 · D1 – ARDS: Berlin vs new global definition, pathophysiology, phenotypes

**1.** A 58-year-old with pneumococcal pneumonia is on high-flow nasal oxygen 50 L/min, FiO₂ 0.7, PaO₂ 63 mmHg (SpO₂ 92 %). Chest radiograph shows bilateral opacities; echocardiography is normal. Which statement is correct?

- A. He meets the new global definition of ARDS (non-intubated category)
- B. He meets the Berlin definition of moderate ARDS
- C. ARDS cannot be diagnosed without a CT scan
- D. He cannot have ARDS until he is intubated
- E. He meets the global definition of severe ARDS

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** P/F = 63/0.7 = 90; HFNO ≥ 30 L/min meets the global definition (non-intubated). Berlin does not allow HFNO; non-intubated patients are not stratified by severity.

</details>

**2.** Regarding the Berlin definition of ARDS:

- A. The term "acute lung injury" was abandoned. — T / F
- B. Four ancillary variables for severe ARDS improved predictive validity and were retained. — T / F
- C. A minimum PEEP or CPAP of 10 cmH₂O is required when assessing PaO₂/FiO₂. — T / F
- D. A pulmonary artery wedge pressure ≤ 18 mmHg is required. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – F.** D: PCWP was removed. B: they added no predictive value and were dropped. C: the minimum is PEEP/CPAP ≥ 5 cmH₂O.

</details>

**3.** In the new global definition of ARDS, the SpO₂/FiO₂ ratio may replace PaO₂/FiO₂ only if:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Above 97% S/F is not valid (flat part of the dissociation curve).

</details>

**4.** Regarding the modification of the global ARDS definition for resource-limited settings:

**O1. (open)** A patient with pneumonia is on HFNO 40 L/min, FiO₂ 0.6, PaO₂ 75 mmHg, with bilateral B-lines on lung ultrasound and a normal echocardiogram. Calculate the P/F ratio and explain whether the patient meets the Berlin and the new global definitions of ARDS.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- P/F = 75 / 0.6 = 125 mmHg.
- Berlin: requires PEEP/CPAP ≥ 5 cmH₂O; HFNO has no defined PEEP → Berlin criteria not met.
- Global definition 2024: non-intubated category allows HFNO ≥ 30 L/min with P/F ≤ 300 (or S/F ≤ 315 if SpO₂ ≤ 97%) → met.
- Imaging criterion satisfied by bilateral B-lines/consolidations on lung ultrasound (trained operator).
- Risk factor present (pneumonia), onset within 1 week, oedema not primarily cardiogenic (normal echo).
- Non-intubated patients are not stratified into mild/moderate/severe.
- On HFNO < 30 L/min the global definition would not be met (outside the resource-limited category).

</details>

**O2. (open)** Explain the "baby lung" concept and its implications for setting tidal volume in ARDS.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- In severe ARDS the normally aerated part of the lung is the size of a small child's lung.
- Low compliance reflects a small amount of aerated lung rather than intrinsically stiff lung.
- VT scaled to predicted body weight may still be excessive for a small aerated lung → excessive strain (volutrauma).
- Hence the logic of driving pressure (VT normalised to compliance).
- Gravitational distribution: dorsal atelectasis supine, ventral hyperinflation → rationale for prone positioning.
- VILI mechanisms: volutrauma, barotrauma, atelectrauma, biotrauma (→ MODS).

</details>


## W10 · D2 – Protective ventilation: ARMA, plateau and driving pressure, PBW

**1.** A 165-cm tall woman weighing 95 kg is intubated for ARDS. Her predicted body weight and the corresponding tidal volume at 6 mL/kg PBW are approximately:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** 45.5 + 0.91 × (165 − 152.4) = 45.5 + 11.5 = 57.0 kg; × 6 = 342 mL.

</details>

- A. Mortality was 31.0 % vs 39.8 % in favour of low tidal volume. — T / F
- B. The trial was stopped early for futility. — T / F
- C. The low tidal volume group was ventilated with 6 mL/kg actual body weight and plateau pressure ≤ 30 cmH₂O. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – F, D – T.** B: stopped for benefit. C: tidal volume was calculated from predicted, not actual, body weight.

</details>

**3.** A ventilated ARDS patient has VT 450 mL, PEEP 10 cmH₂O, peak pressure 38 cmH₂O and plateau pressure 28 cmH₂O. Which statement is correct?

- A. Driving pressure is 28 cmH₂O
- B. Driving pressure is 18 cmH₂O and static compliance is 25 mL/cmH₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – F.** D: RR 1.36 even in "protected" patients.

</details>

**5.** According to the ESICM 2023 ARDS guidelines, the tidal volume recommendation for ARDS not due to COVID-19 is:

**6.** Regarding plateau pressure measurement:

- A. It requires an end-inspiratory pause in a patient without active breathing effort. — T / F
- B. Active inspiratory effort may cause plateau pressure to underestimate lung stress. — T / F

**7.** A 180-cm man with ARDS is ventilated with VT 600 mL. His driving pressure is 19 cmH₂O, plateau pressure 31 cmH₂O. The most appropriate first change is:

**O1. (open)** A 170-cm man with moderate ARDS is ventilated in volume control with VT 560 mL, PEEP 8 cmH₂O, Ppeak 36 cmH₂O and Pplat 30 cmH₂O. Calculate his PBW, VT per kg PBW, driving pressure and static compliance, interpret the values and outline the changes you would make.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- PBW = 50 + 0.91 × (170 − 152.4) = 66.0 kg; VT 560 mL ≈ 8.5 mL/kg PBW (above the 4–8 mL/kg range; 6 mL/kg ≈ 396 mL).
- ΔP = 30 − 8 = 22 cmH₂O (well above ~15); Crs = 560/22 ≈ 25 mL/cmH₂O.
- Ppeak − Pplat = 6 cmH₂O = resistive component.
- Pplat is at the 30 cmH₂O limit, but a high ΔP means ventilation is not "safe" despite meeting the Pplat limit.
- Reduce VT towards 6 mL/kg PBW (down to 4 mL/kg if Pplat remains > 30), increase rate (up to 35/min) to maintain pH ≥ 7.30; accept permissive hypercapnia (caution with raised ICP/RV failure).
- Recheck Pplat with an inspiratory pause in a relaxed patient, measure total PEEP (end-expiratory hold); reassess ΔP and compliance after each change.

</details>

**O2. (open)** Explain why driving pressure may be a better marker of lung stress than VT/PBW, and why it is not yet a guideline recommendation.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- ΔP = Pplat − PEEP = VT/Crs, i.e. VT normalised to the size of the functional (baby) lung → reflects strain.
- Amato 2015: individual data of 3562 patients from 9 RCTs; ΔP had the strongest association with survival (RR 1.41 per 1 SD ~7 cmH₂O).
- Association persisted even with "protective" Pplat and VT (RR 1.36).
- Changes in VT or PEEP were associated with survival only if they reduced ΔP.
- However, it is a mediation analysis, not an RCT; the ~15 cmH₂O threshold comes from observational data.
- No prospective RCT targeting ΔP; ESICM 2023 gives no recommendation (research question).

</details>

**O1. (open)** A patient with moderate ARDS (P/F 140) is ventilated with VT 6 mL/kg PBW. Outline how you would set and titrate PEEP, and how you would recognise recruitment versus overdistension at the bedside.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Start with PEEP from the PEEP/FiO₂ table, rather the higher end with P/F ≤ 200 (Briel: benefit of higher PEEP only at P/F ≤ 200).
- Assess the response after each PEEP change: ΔP/compliance, P/F, PaCO₂, haemodynamics.
- Recruitment: ΔP falls (compliance rises), oxygenation improves, no haemodynamic deterioration.
- Overdistension/non-recruitable: ΔP and Pplat rise, PaCO₂ rises (↑ dead space), BP falls (↓ venous return, ↑ RV afterload) → return to the previous PEEP.
- No routine recruitment manoeuvres; avoid prolonged high-pressure RM (ART: ↑ mortality and barotrauma; ESICM strong against).
- Consider oesophageal pressure in obesity/IAH/stiff chest wall.
- With P/F < 150 → prone positioning, which has mortality benefit unlike PEEP.

</details>

**O2. (open)** Explain why ART showed harm while LOVS and EXPRESS were neutral, and how ESICM 2023 and ATS 2024 differ in their PEEP recommendations.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- LOVS (RM 40 cmH₂O × 40 s, high PEEP) and EXPRESS (PEEP to Pplat 28–30) did not reduce mortality but improved secondary outcomes (less refractory hypoxaemia/rescue therapies; more ventilator-free and organ failure-free days).
- ART used a maximal RM (PEEP up to 45, peak up to 60 cmH₂O) plus decremental titration in an unselected population; 28-d mortality 55.3 vs 49.3% (HR 1.20).
- Harm likely due to haemodynamic instability and barotrauma (5.6 vs 1.6%; pneumothorax requiring drainage 3.2 vs 1.2%); protocol softened mid-trial for safety.
- Recruitability varies widely; focal ARDS tends to overdistend with high pressures.
- ESICM 2023: no recommendation for/against higher PEEP or mechanics-guided titration; strong against prolonged RM; conditional against routine brief RM.
- ATS 2024: conditionally suggests higher PEEP without RM in moderate–severe ARDS; strong against prolonged RM.

</details>

**O1. (open)** A 50-year-old with pneumonia-related ARDS has P/F 110 on FiO₂ 0.7 and PEEP 12 cmH₂O, 18 h after intubation, despite VT 6 mL/kg PBW. Outline your management regarding prone positioning, neuromuscular blockade and corticosteroids, justifying each choice with the evidence.

**O2. (open)** Explain why ACURASYS and ROSE reached different conclusions on neuromuscular blockade in ARDS.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:

</details>
