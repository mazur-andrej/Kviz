**1.** A 67-year-old woman with pyelonephritis remains hypotensive after 30 mL/kg of crystalloid and needs noradrenaline 0.08 µg/kg/min to keep MAP at 65 mmHg. Lactate is 3.4 mmol/L. Which statement is correct?

- A. She has sepsis but not septic shock, because lactate is below 4 mmol/L
- B. She meets the Sepsis-3 criteria for septic shock, a state associated with hospital mortality > 40 % in the derivation cohorts
- C. She has "severe sepsis"
- D. Septic shock can only be diagnosed if qSOFA is ≥ 2
- E. Septic shock requires MAP < 65 mmHg despite vasopressors

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** Sepsis-3 defines septic shock as a vasopressor requirement to keep MAP ≥ 65 mmHg **and** lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation – she meets both. In the derivation cohorts this combination carried hospital mortality > 40 %.

**Why not the others:**

- A – The 4 mmol/L threshold comes from pre-Sepsis-3 definitions; the current cut-off is > 2 mmol/L.
- C – The term "severe sepsis" was abolished by Sepsis-3 in 2016.
- D – qSOFA is a prognostic bedside prompt and is not part of any sepsis or septic shock definition.
- E – The criterion is needing vasopressors *to achieve* MAP ≥ 65 mmHg, not failing to reach it.

**EDIC trap:** "lactate > 4" and "severe sepsis" are Sepsis-1/2 distractors.

**Ref:** Singer M, et al. Sepsis-3. *JAMA* 2016;315:801–810; Shankar-Hari M, et al. *JAMA* 2016;315:775–787.

**2.** Regarding the timing of antimicrobials and cultures in SSC 2026:

- A. In probable sepsis without shock, antimicrobials within 1 hour is a conditional recommendation. — T / F
- B. Patients with sepsis requiring ICU care should be admitted to the ICU within 6 hours. — T / F
- C. In possible sepsis without shock, if concern for infection persists after rapid investigation, antimicrobials should be given within 6 hours of first suspicion. — T / F
- D. Blood cultures should be obtained before antimicrobials if this does not substantially delay them. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – T, C – F, D – T.**

- A (False) – For probable or definite sepsis without shock, antimicrobials within 1 h is a **strong** recommendation (very low certainty), as for shock.
- B (True) – Patients needing ICU care should be admitted within 6 h (conditional, low certainty); delayed transfer is associated with worse outcome.
- C (False) – For possible sepsis without shock the window is **3 h** if concern persists after rapid assessment; with a low likelihood of infection, deferral with close monitoring is suggested.
- D (True) – Cultures, including blood cultures, are taken before antimicrobials when this causes no substantial delay.

**EDIC trap:** a strong recommendation can rest on very low certainty evidence.

**Ref:** Prescott HC, et al. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med* 2026 / *Crit Care Med* 2026;54:725–812.

**3.** A 50-year-old has septic shock from a perforated sigmoid diverticulitis. Which statement about initial management is most consistent with SSC 2026?

- A. Source control should be postponed until 48 hours of antibiotics have been given
- B. Procalcitonin should be measured to decide whether to start antibiotics
- C. Empirical anaerobic coverage is suggested because an intra-abdominal source is a risk factor for anaerobic infection
- D. Antibiotics may be given within 3 hours, as the infection source is obvious
- E. Anaerobic coverage should be omitted, as SSC 2026 advises against it in all patients

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** New in SSC 2026: empirical anaerobic coverage is suggested for patients with risk factors for anaerobic infection, such as an intra-abdominal source, and omitted in those without (both conditional, very low certainty). Perforated diverticulitis is the textbook indication.

**Why not the others:**

- A – Source control should be early, ideally within 6 h of diagnosis, not delayed until after 48 h of antibiotics.
- B – Procalcitonin is not used to decide whether to start antimicrobials.
- D – In septic shock antimicrobials are given immediately, ideally within 1 h; the 3-h window applies only to possible sepsis without shock.
- E – SSC 2026 advises against anaerobic coverage only in patients **without** risk factors.

**4.** Comparing the CLASSIC and CLOVERS trials:

- A. In CLASSIC, 90-day mortality was about 42 % in both arms. — T / F
- B. In CLOVERS, the restrictive and liberal arms differed by roughly 2 L of fluid. — T / F
- C. In CLOVERS, serious adverse events were similar with the restrictive and liberal strategies. — T / F
- D. CLASSIC randomised ICU patients with septic shock after initial resuscitation with at least 1 L of fluid. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – T, D – T.**

- A (True) – CLASSIC (1554 ICU patients with septic shock): 90-day mortality 42.3 % (restrictive) vs 42.1 % (standard).

**5.** A 58-year-old with alcoholic cirrhosis and septic shock has already received 4 L of balanced crystalloid. Further fluid is judged necessary. Which choice is most consistent with SSC 2026?

- B. 20 % albumin is contraindicated in cirrhosis
- C. Switch to 0.9 % saline because balanced solutions contain potassium

**7.** The new SSC 2026 suggestion of an initial MAP target of 60–65 mmHg in patients aged ≥ 65 years is based principally on which trial?

- A. SOAP II
- B. VASST
- C. ATHOS-3
- D. The 65 trial
- E. SEPSISPAM

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** The 65 trial (Lamontagne, JAMA 2020) randomised 2600 patients aged ≥ 65 years with vasodilatory hypotension to permissive MAP 60–65 mmHg or usual care. 90-day mortality was 41.0 % vs 43.8 % (NS) with less vasopressor exposure and no signal of harm, which underpins the SSC 2026 suggestion (conditional, low).

**9.** In which trial did hydrocortisone significantly reduce 90-day all-cause mortality in septic shock?

- A. CAPE COD
- B. CORTICUS
- C. VANISH
- D. ADRENAL
- E. APROCCHSS

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** APROCCHSS (1241 patients) gave hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h plus fludrocortisone 50 µg for 7 days; 90-day mortality was 43.0 % vs 49.1 %, significantly lower. It is the large septic shock trial with a significant 90-day mortality benefit.

**Why not the others:**

**10.** Regarding lactate in septic shock:

- A. An adrenaline infusion may raise lactate through β2-stimulated aerobic glycolysis. — T / F
- B. Lactate responds within minutes, so it is ideal for judging the effect of a single fluid bolus. — T / F
- C. SSC 2026 advises against continuing fluids solely because lactate has not yet normalised. — T / F
- D. Lactate > 4 mmol/L is one of the two clinical criteria of septic shock. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

**11.** Besides hyperchloraemic acidosis, which mechanism is proposed for the renal harm associated with large volumes of 0.9 % saline?

- A. Direct tubular toxicity of sodium
- B. An increase in the plasma strong ion difference
- C. Hyperkalaemia caused by the potassium content of saline
- D. Saline-induced hypoalbuminaemic alkalosis
- E. Renal vasoconstriction through tubuloglomerular feedback triggered by chloride at the macula densa

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**E.**

**Why E:** 0.9 % saline contains 154 mmol/L Cl⁻ (plasma ~100). Besides lowering the SID (hyperchloraemic acidosis), high chloride delivery to the macula densa triggers tubuloglomerular feedback with afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction and a fall in GFR.

**Why not the others:**

- A – Sodium is not directly tubulotoxic; the proposed mediator is chloride.
- B – Saline has an SID of zero and therefore **lowers** plasma SID.
- C – Saline contains no potassium.
- D – Dilutional hypoalbuminaemia is mildly alkalinising, but it is not a mechanism of renal harm.

**Ref:** Wilcox CS. *J Clin Invest* 1983;71:726–735; Semler MW, et al. SMART. *N Engl J Med* 2018;378:829–839.

- C (False) – Use permissive hypercapnia; raising RR shortens Te and worsens dynamic hyperinflation.
- D (False) – A high Ppeak with an acceptable Pplat is the resistive component, while Pplat (with PEEPi) reflects alveolar pressure.

**EDIC trap:** RR is the most powerful lever for lengthening Te.

**Ref:** Tuxen DV, Lane S. *Am Rev Respir Dis* 1987;136:872–879; Williams TJ, et al. *Am Rev Respir Dis* 1992;146:607–615.

**63.** A ventilated asthmatic is on VCV with V_T 600 mL, constant inspiratory flow 60 L/min with no inspiratory pause, and RR 15/min. What are the inspiratory time, expiratory time and I:E ratio?

- A. Ti 0.36 s; Te 3.64 s; I:E 1:10
- B. Ti 0.6 s; Te 3.4 s; I:E ≈ 1:5.7
- C. Ti 0.6 s; Te 2.4 s; I:E 1:4
- D. Ti 1.0 s; Te 3.0 s; I:E 1:3
- E. Ti 0.6 s; Te 3.4 s; I:E 1:3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

**Why B:** The cycle is 60/15 = 4 s. Ti = V_T/flow = 0.6 L ÷ 1 L/s = 0.6 s, so Te = 4 − 0.6 = 3.4 s and I:E = 0.6:3.4 ≈ 1:5.7.

**Why not the others:**
- A – Ti 0.36 s does not fit V_T 0.6 L at 1 L/s.
- C – Te 2.4 s corresponds to a 3-s cycle (RR 20/min).
- D – Ti 1.0 s would require a flow of 36 L/min.
- E – Ti and Te are correct, but the I:E ratio is 1:5.7, not 1:3.

**EDIC trap:** Convert flow from L/min to L/s (60 L/min = 1 L/s).

**65.** A 180-cm man with ARDS is ventilated on VCV with V_T 450 mL. Pplat is 30 cmH₂O, PEEP is 14 cmH₂O and there is no intrinsic PEEP. What are his predicted body weight and respiratory-system compliance?

- A. PBW 80 kg; C_rs ≈ 28 mL/cmH₂O
- B. PBW 75 kg; C_rs ≈ 15 mL/cmH₂O
- C. PBW 70 kg; C_rs ≈ 32 mL/cmH₂O
- D. PBW ≈ 75 kg; C_rs ≈ 28 mL/cmH₂O
- E. PBW 75 kg; C_rs ≈ 45 mL/cmH₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.**

**Why D:** PBW (men) = 50 + 0.91 × (180 − 152.4) = 50 + 25.1 ≈ 75 kg, so 450 mL ≈ 6 mL/kg PBW. ΔP = Pplat − PEEP = 30 − 14 = 16 cmH₂O, and C_rs = 450/16 ≈ 28 mL/cmH₂O.

**Why not the others:**
- A – C_rs is right, but PBW is 75 kg, not 80 kg.
- B – 15 mL/cmH₂O divides V_T by Pplat instead of by ΔP.
- C – PBW is 75 kg, and 32 mL/cmH₂O divides V_T by PEEP (450/14).
- E – 45 mL/cmH₂O is a miscalculation; ΔP is 16 cmH₂O.

**EDIC trap:** Compliance uses driving pressure (Pplat − total PEEP), not Pplat.

**67.** A COPD patient on PSV 18 cmH₂O (cycle-off 25 %) shows a pressure spike at the end of inspiration and frequent ineffective efforts during expiration. Which adjustment is most appropriate?

- B. Switch to SIMV
- C. Increase cycle-off to 40–50 % and reduce pressure support
- D. Decrease cycle-off to 10 %
- E. Make the trigger less sensitive

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- B – SIMV does not correct delayed cycling and prolongs weaning.

**69.** A patient with ARDS is on VCV with V_T 0.42 L, RR 30/min, Ppeak 34 cmH₂O, Pplat 28 cmH₂O and PEEP 12 cmH₂O. The team plans to raise RR to 35/min for hypercapnia. Using MP ≈ 0.098 × RR × V_T × (Ppeak − ½ΔP), which statement is correct?

- A. MP ≈ 16.1 J/min, within the range not associated with excess mortality
- B. MP ≈ 32.1 J/min, and raising RR will reduce it
- C. MP ≈ 32.1 J/min, well above ~17 J/min; raising RR will increase it further
- D. MP ≈ 64 J/min
- E. MP ≈ 12.6 J/min

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

**Why C:** ΔP = 28 − 12 = 16 cmH₂O, so ½ΔP = 8. MP = 0.098 × 30 × 0.42 × (34 − 8) = 1.2348 × 26 ≈ 32.1 J/min, well above the ~17 J/min associated with mortality in observational data. MP rises with RR (exponent 1.4), so RR 35/min will increase it further.

- B – The value is correct, but raising RR increases MP rather than reducing it.
- D – 64 J/min is double the correct value.
- E – 12.6 J/min is a miscalculation.

**EDIC trap:** The driving pressure of 16 cmH₂O is also high; MP is an observational marker, not a guideline target.
