## W13 · D1 – Ventilation modes: VCV, PCV, PRVC, PSV, SIMV, APRV, NAVA, PAV

**1.** A patient on volume-controlled ventilation with constant inspiratory flow of 60 L/min, VT 500 mL and PEEP 5 cmH₂O has a respiratory system compliance of 25 mL/cmH₂O and an airway resistance of 10 cmH₂O/L/s. The expected peak and plateau pressures are:

- A. Ppeak 25, Pplat 20 cmH₂O
- B. Ppeak 45, Pplat 35 cmH₂O
- C. Ppeak 35, Pplat 25 cmH₂O
- D. Ppeak 35, Pplat 30 cmH₂O
- E. Ppeak 30, Pplat 25 cmH₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.**

Pplat = 5 + 500/25 = 25; Ppeak = 25 + 10 × 1 L/s = 35 cmH₂O.

</details>

**11.** A patient with ARDS is on APRV (P-high 28 cmH₂O, T-high 5 s, P-low 0 cmH₂O, T-low 0.8 s). On the flow–time curve the expiratory flow during each release peaks at 60 L/min and decays to 20 L/min before pressure returns to P-high. According to the approach described in BJA Education (2020), the most appropriate adjustment is:

- A. Add pressure support of 10 cmH₂O to spontaneous breaths
- B. Shorten T-low (in 0.1-s steps) until the release ends at about 45 L/min
- C. Lengthen T-low to 1.2 s so that expiratory flow reaches zero
- D. Shorten T-high to 2 s to increase the number of releases
- E. Increase P-low to 10 cmH₂O and leave T-low unchanged

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

The release should be terminated at ~75 % of peak expiratory flow (0.75 × 60 = 45 L/min). Flow falling to 20 L/min (33 %) means T-low is too long → risk of derecruitment; shorten T-low by 0.1 s at a time. Allowing flow to reach zero (C) defeats the intended auto-PEEP; added PS (A) may over-distend recruited lung.

</details>

**12.** Regarding airway pressure release ventilation (APRV):

- A. Severe bronchospasm is a relative contraindication because a short T-low cannot adequately empty slow lung units. — T / F
- B. Spontaneous inspiratory efforts taken at P-high may generate high transpulmonary pressures, particularly in heterogeneous lungs. — T / F
- C. APRV is recommended in preference to prone positioning for severe ARDS. — T / F
- D. In the trial by Zhou et al. (2017), patients on APRV were less often successfully extubated than those on low tidal volume ventilation. — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – T, B – T, C – F, D – F.**

D: more often – 66.2 % vs 38.8 %. C: prone positioning has robust mortality evidence and should come first; APRV is considered if prone is contraindicated or fails (Swindin, BJA Educ 2020).

</details>

**O1. (open)** A patient with recovering ARDS is switched from VCV to pressure control (Pinsp 15 cmH₂O above PEEP, C 40 mL/cmH₂O) and starts making strong inspiratory efforts (estimated P_mus ≈ 10 cmH₂O). Calculate the expected tidal volume with and without effort, and explain why a "low" displayed pressure may be misleading. How would you monitor this patient?

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Equation of motion: in pressure-targeted modes VT = (Pinsp + P_mus) × C.
- Passive: VT ≈ 15 × 40 = 600 mL; with effort: (15 + 10) × 40 ≈ 1000 mL.
- The displayed airway pressure stays at 15 cmH₂O – the ventilator holds the set pressure; effort increases VT, not P_aw.
- Transpulmonary pressure = ventilator pressure + P_mus → hidden volutrauma / P-SILI risk.
- Monitor VT (not just pressure) and estimates of effort/drive: P0.1, Pocc, Pes (Brochard 2017).
- Also note: a fall in compliance in PCV causes a fall in VT → low VE alarm is mandatory.
- If Ti < ~3τ, pressure does not equilibrate and VT is smaller than Pinsp × C.

</details>

**O2. (open)** Compare the evidence for SIMV, PSV and a daily spontaneous breathing trial as weaning strategies, and explain the physiological reason SIMV performs poorly.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- Esteban (NEJM 1995): 546 patients, 130 randomised after failing a 2-h SBT.
- Median weaning duration: IMV 5 d, PSV 4 d, multiple SBTs 3 d, once-daily SBT 3 d.
- Once-daily SBT weaned patients 2.83× faster than IMV and 2.05× faster than PSV.
- SIMV mixes supported mandatory and unsupported/partially supported spontaneous breaths.
- Respiratory muscles cannot adapt breath by breath → effort remains high even during mandatory breaths.
- Conclusion: SIMV is the slowest weaning method; the preferred approach is a daily SBT (T-piece or low PS).

</details>


## W13 · D2 – Waveforms, loops and respiratory mechanics: compliance, resistance, τ, pauses, oesophageal pressure

**1.** A sedated, paralysed patient on volume control (constant flow 60 L/min, VT 450 mL, PEEP 8 cmH₂O, total PEEP 8 cmH₂O) has Ppeak 38 cmH₂O and Pplat 26 cmH₂O. Which pair of values is correct?

- A. Static compliance 25 mL/cmH₂O; airway resistance 12 cmH₂O/L/s
- B. Static compliance 17 mL/cmH₂O; airway resistance 12 cmH₂O/L/s
- C. Static compliance 25 mL/cmH₂O; airway resistance 0.2 cmH₂O/L/s
- D. Static compliance 15 mL/cmH₂O; airway resistance 26 cmH₂O/L/s
- E. Static compliance 30 mL/cmH₂O; airway resistance 6 cmH₂O/L/s

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.**

C_stat = 450/(26 − 8) = 450/18 = 25 mL/cmH₂O; R = (38 − 26)/1 L/s = 12 cmH₂O/L/s. A value of 15 mL/cmH₂O would be C_dyn = 450/(38 − 8).

</details>

**2.** A ventilated patient's peak pressure rises suddenly from 28 to 42 cmH₂O while the plateau pressure stays at 22 cmH₂O. Possible causes include:

- A. Tension pneumothorax — T / F
- B. Endobronchial (right main bronchus) intubation — T / F
- C. Kinked or bitten endotracheal tube — T / F
- D. Abdominal distension — T / F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A – F, B – F, C – T, D – F.**

Unchanged Pplat = a resistance problem (tube kinking, secretions, bronchospasm). Pneumothorax, endobronchial intubation and abdominal distension reduce compliance and raise Pplat.

</details>

**3.** A patient has airway resistance of 20 cmH₂O/L/s and respiratory system compliance of 50 mL/cmH₂O. Approximately how long an expiratory time is needed to exhale about 95 % of the tidal volume?

- E. 1.0 s

**B.**

τ = 20 × 0.05 = 1.0 s; 95 % ≈ 3τ = 3.0 s.

</details>

**5.** Dynamic compliance is lower than static compliance in a patient because:

**O1. (open)** A paralysed patient on VCV (VT 500 mL, constant flow 60 L/min, PEEP_set 5) has Ppeak 35, Pplat 25 and PEEP_tot 5 cmH₂O. Calculate static and dynamic compliance, driving pressure, airway resistance and the time constant, and interpret whether expiratory time is adequate. Then explain how you would distinguish a resistance problem from a compliance problem if Ppeak suddenly rose.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- C_stat = 500/(25 − 5) = 25 mL/cmH₂O; C_dyn = 500/(35 − 5) = 16.7 mL/cmH₂O (C_dyn ≤ C_stat because it includes resistance).
- ΔP = 25 − 5 = 20 cmH₂O.
- R_aw = (35 − 25)/1 L/s = 10 cmH₂O/L/s – valid only with constant flow; flow converted to L/s.
- τ = 10 × 0.025 = 0.25 s; 3τ = 0.75 s (~95 % exhaled) → a 2-s expiration is sufficient.
- Use PEEP_tot (end-expiratory hold) rather than set PEEP; measurements require a passive patient.
- If Ppeak rises with unchanged Pplat → ↑ resistance (bronchospasm, secretions, kinked/bitten tube).
- If Ppeak and Pplat both rise → ↓ compliance (pneumothorax, endobronchial intubation, atelectasis, oedema, ↑ IAP, dynamic hyperinflation).

</details>

**O2. (open)** An obese patient (PEEP 12 cmH₂O) has an end-expiratory oesophageal pressure of 17 cmH₂O and Pplat 32 cmH₂O. Interpret these values and discuss the role of oesophageal pressure monitoring in light of Talmor (2008) and EPVent-2 (2019).

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Key points expected:
- P_L = P_aw − P_es; P_L,ee = 12 − 17 = −5 cmH₂O → dependent regions collapse at end-expiration (atelectrauma).
- Increasing PEEP to achieve P_L,ee ≥ 0 is physiologically justified.
- With a stiff chest wall a Pplat of 32 does not necessarily mean excessive lung stress (E_L/E_rs varies 0.33–0.95, Chiumello).
- Correct balloon position: lower third of oesophagus, occlusion test ΔP_es/ΔP_aw ≈ 0.8–1.2, cardiac oscillations.
- Talmor (EPVent, 61 patients): P/F 88 mmHg higher at 72 h, better compliance; not powered for mortality.
- EPVent-2 (200 patients): no difference in death + ventilator-free days; 28-d mortality 32.4 vs 30.6 %.
- Conclusion: not routine; useful physiologically in obesity, ↑ IAP, stiff chest wall, high patient effort.

</details>


## W13 · D3 – Patient–ventilator asynchronies

**11.** A patient with pneumonia (respiratory system compliance 28 mL/cmH₂O) is on PSV 8 cmH₂O, PEEP 8 cmH₂O, cycle-off 40 %, rate 32/min. The flow–time curve shows, immediately after the peak expiratory flow, a small positive bump with an abrupt slowing of expiratory flow; some breaths are followed within 0.3 s by a second triggered breath. The most appropriate adjustment is:

**13.** A patient with COPD on non-invasive pressure support (IPAP 16, EPAP 5 cmH₂O, ETS 25 %) with a moderate mask leak has long inspirations, an end-inspiratory pressure spike and visible ineffective efforts during expiration. The most appropriate cycling adjustment is:

- A. Increase the rise time to the slowest setting and leave ETS unchanged

Lowering ETS (B) or raising IPAP (E) prolongs inspiration and worsens hyperinflation; a less sensitive trigger (C) increases ineffective efforts.

**15.** In the study by Serpa Neto et al. (ICM 2018), mechanical power:

- A. Showed no relation with mortality
- B. Was associated with in-hospital mortality per 5 J/min increase, with risk rising above about 17 J/min
- C. Was relevant only when tidal volume exceeded 10 mL/kg PBW
- D. Was a randomised intervention target
- E. Was calculated only in patients on ECMO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

Each +5 J/min increased the odds of death; association also present with low VT.

</details>
